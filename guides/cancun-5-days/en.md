# Start here | The plan at a glance

The mistake most visitors make here is trying to see everything. The distances are larger than the map suggests: Chichén Itzá is two and a half hours from Cancún, Tulum two hours, and two long days in a row will wear you out. With five days, spend three on the beach and two on trips: one long day to Chichén Itzá with a cenote swim, and one shorter day to Isla Mujeres or the Tulum ruins, whichever is closer to your hotel. Put a beach day between them.

| Day | Plan | The big booking | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive, transfer, beach until sunset | Airport transfer | Easy |
| 2 | Beach day, local snorkeling | None | Easy |
| 3 | Chichén Itzá, cenote, Valladolid | Chichén Itzá tour or rental car | Long: 12 hours |
| 4 | Recover: beach or pool | None | Easy |
| 5 | Isla Mujeres (from Cancún) or Tulum ruins and a cenote (from the Riviera Maya) | Ferry or early start | Medium |

:::booking Book in this order
1. **Before you fly:** a [private airport transfer](book:Cancun airport transfer). About $35 to $55 per vehicle to the Cancún Hotel Zone, $50 to $80 to Playa del Carmen. It is the single booking that most improves day 1.
2. **Before you fly, or on day 1:** an [early-access or small-group Chichén Itzá tour](book:Chichen Itza tour from Cancun) for day 3. Prices at the hotel tour desk are usually higher than online.
3. **A few days ahead, if you're traveling with children:** [Xcaret](book:Xcaret park tickets) as a swap for day 5. $100 to $160 per adult; plan to stay until the evening show ends.
4. **The day before is fine:** an [Isla Mujeres catamaran](book:Isla Mujeres catamaran from Cancun) if you want sailing and snorkeling more than beach time, or a [Tulum and cenote tour](book:Tulum ruins tour cenote) if you'd rather not go on your own.
5. **Online, any time before you leave Mexico:** the Visitax, the state tourist tax of about 290 pesos per person, on the official state website only.
:::

:::warn Three things that catch people out
- **The arrivals hall.** Between customs and the exit, people in uniforms offer transport, tours and "help". They sell timeshare presentations and overpriced rides. Do not stop: your real driver is outside the building, holding a sign.
- **The time zone.** Chichén Itzá is one hour behind Cancún. Tours account for it; if you drive, a 7 am departure gets you there soon after it opens at 8 am local time.
- **Look-alike websites** for the Visitax charge more for the same payment. Check the address before you enter your card.
:::

## Where to stay decides your trip

:::map The coast from north to south
- **Cancún Hotel Zone:** A 14-mile strip of big resorts between the sea and a lagoon. The widest beaches, the most nightlife, the closest to the airport (20 to 30 minutes) and to the Isla Mujeres ferry. In March it fills with spring breakers.
- **Playa Mujeres and Costa Mujeres:** North of Cancún. Newer, quieter all-inclusives, and usually less seaweed.
- **Puerto Morelos:** A fishing town between Cancún and Playa del Carmen, with a reef close to shore.
- **Playa del Carmen:** A walkable town 45 to 60 minutes from the airport, with the ferry to Cozumel. Central for both Chichén Itzá and Tulum.
- **Tulum:** About 2 hours from Cancún airport, though it now has its own international airport 45 minutes from town. Boutique hotels, the ruins and the best cenotes nearby. Tulum town, the beach hotel strip and the ruins are three different places several miles apart.
:::

**Seaweed matters.** From roughly April to August sargassum washes ashore, and 2026 was reported as the worst season on record. The coast from Playa del Carmen to Tulum is usually hit hardest; Cancún's north-facing beaches and Isla Mujeres stay clearer. In those months pick a hotel with good pools.

# Getting there | Flights, the airport and the tourist tax

## Flying from the US

Cancún (CUN) is one of the best-connected airports in the Americas, with nonstops from most large US cities: about 2 hours from Miami, 2.5 from Houston and Dallas, 4 from New York and Chicago, 5 from Los Angeles. Cancún is on Eastern Standard Time all year and does not change its clocks, so in summer it is an hour behind New York.

- **Passport:** You need a valid passport book. Keep any entry paper or stamp you are given until you leave.
- **Tulum airport:** If your hotel is in Tulum or the southern Riviera Maya, a flight there saves most of the road transfer.

## From Cancún airport to your hotel

| Option | Price | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| [Private transfer](book:Cancun airport transfer), Hotel Zone | About $35 to $55 per vehicle | Families and groups; no stops; child seats on request |
| Private transfer, Playa del Carmen | About $50 to $80 per vehicle | The same, 45 to 60 minutes |
| ADO bus to downtown Cancún | About 140 pesos | Solo travelers and couples; add a short taxi to the Hotel Zone |
| ADO bus to Playa del Carmen | About 220 pesos | Budget travelers staying in Playa |
| Shared shuttle | Less than private | Patient people: it drops others first and can take twice as long |
| Airport taxi | Fixed rates at a booth | Nobody, if you can avoid it: usually the most expensive |

If you booked nothing, the ADO ticket counter is outside the terminal, to the right. The buses are air-conditioned coaches and leave from every terminal.

## The Visitax

The state of Quintana Roo charges foreign visitors a tourist tax of about 290 pesos (roughly $16) per person. Pay it on the official state website before or during your trip, and keep the QR code on your phone; you may be asked for it at the airport when you leave.

# Day 1 | Arrive and settle

:::schedule
Landing | Straight through the arrivals hall
Collect bags, clear customs, keep walking. Outside, find the sign with your name.

Check-in | Pesos and water
Withdraw pesos from a bank ATM (not a freestanding machine in a shop) and decline the machine's own conversion rate. Drink bottled or filtered water; resorts provide it.

Afternoon | Beach or pool until sunset
Nothing else. The sun is strong even late in the day: sunscreen and a hat from the first hour.

Evening | Book the rest of the week
If you haven't yet, book day 3 online tonight.
:::

:::tip Pay in pesos, not dollars
Shops and taxis that accept dollars use an exchange rate that costs you about ten percent. Pesos are also what you need for entry fees, local buses and tips.
:::

# Day 2 | Beach day

A full day of doing little, with one optional outing close to home.

:::schedule
Morning | The beach before the heat
The sand is at its best before 11 am. If seaweed has come in, ask the hotel which nearby beach is clear.

Midday | Shade and lunch
From noon to 3 pm the sun is fierce. Eat slowly.

Afternoon | One small outing
See the options below, matched to where you are staying.

Sunset | A drink on the beach
:::

## An easy outing near your hotel

- **From Cancún:** Playa Delfines, the public beach in the Hotel Zone with no hotel behind it, free palapas and the Cancún sign. A local bus runs the length of the strip.
- **From Cancún or Playa del Carmen:** Puerto Morelos. Snorkel trips to the reef leave from the pier and take about two hours.
- **From Playa del Carmen or Tulum:** Akumal, a bay where green sea turtles feed on the seagrass. Snorkeling is regulated to protect them, so go with a licensed guide.
- **From anywhere south of Playa:** the open-air cenotes near Puerto Aventuras: clear water, platforms to jump from, few tour groups on a weekday morning. About $5 to $25 each, in pesos.

# Day 3 | Chichén Itzá

The long day. One of the great Maya cities, a swim in a cenote and, on the better tours, the colonial town of Valladolid. Go as early as you can: the site is hot and crowded by 11 am, when the standard buses arrive.

:::schedule
5:00 to 7:00 am | Pickup
The earlier the tour, the cooler and emptier the site. Tours from the Riviera Maya start earlier and return later than tours from Cancún. Wear a swimsuit under your clothes and bring a towel.

8:00 to 9:00 am (local time) | Arrive at Chichén Itzá
Entry for foreign visitors is about 680 to 700 pesos ($35 to $40), paid at the gate; check whether your tour price includes it. Walk the open plaza and the great pyramid first, before the heat builds. Then the ball court, the Temple of the Warriors and the sacred cenote. Two to three hours.

Noon | Cenote swim and lunch
Most tours stop at one. Shower before you get in and skip regular sunscreen.

Mid-afternoon | Valladolid
Pastel streets, a shaded main square and a cenote in the middle of town. If your tour doesn't include it and you care about seeing a real Yucatán town, choose one that does.

About 7:00 pm | Back at the hotel
Dinner and an early night.
:::

## Tour or drive?

| Option | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Standard group tour | Usually $60 to $120, 12 hours | Easy, but you arrive with every other bus around 11 am |
| [Early-access or small-group tour](book:Chichen Itza tour from Cancun) | More | Leaves around 5 am, arrives at opening. Worth it |
| Rental car | Car plus roughly $40 in tolls | Your own hours; the toll road is fast, straight and safe by day |

**What to check before you book a tour:**

- **Entry fee:** Many cheap tours leave it out.
- **Time on site:** Two hours is the minimum. Some tours spend longer at the buffet and the souvenir stop than at the ruins.
- **Pickup time and group size.**

:::note On site
You cannot climb the pyramid; it has been closed to climbers for years. There is almost no shade: bring a hat and water. Vendors line the paths inside; a polite "no, gracias" is enough. Clap once in front of the main staircase: the echo comes back as a chirp, like the call of the quetzal bird. The site is open from 8 am to 4 pm, with last entry around 3 pm.
:::

:::tip A quieter alternative
With a rental car, link Valladolid, the smaller Maya site of Ek Balam (with remarkably preserved carved stucco) and a cenote. You trade the famous pyramid for a day almost without crowds.
:::

# Day 4 | Recover

:::schedule
All day | Beach or pool
You earned it. If seaweed has come in, spend the day at the pool or ask which beach nearby is clear.

Late afternoon | Get ready for tomorrow
Check ferry times or set the alarm for Tulum. Get pesos for entry fees.
:::

This is also the day for a massage, a long lunch in town or, if you're restless, Playa Delfines or a cenote from the day 2 list.

# Day 5 | Island or ruins

Pick the option nearest your hotel.

## From Cancún: Isla Mujeres by ferry

:::schedule
9:00 am | Taxi to Puerto Juárez
The terminal there is cheaper and has more departures than the piers in the Hotel Zone. The ferry costs about 580 pesos round trip (roughly $30), runs every half hour and takes 20 to 30 minutes.

10:00 am | Playa Norte
A ten-minute walk from the ferry. One of the calmest and shallowest beaches in the region: the water is waist-deep far out. Rent chairs at a beach club or lay a towel on the public sand.

1:00 pm | Lunch in town

2:30 pm | Golf cart to Punta Sur
About $50 to $70 a day; a few hours is enough. Drive to the southern tip for the cliffs and the view back to Cancún.

Evening | Ferry back
The last ferries leave late, so there is no need to rush.
:::

**Ferry or catamaran?** Take the ferry if you want the beach and your own schedule, or have small children. Take a [catamaran tour](book:Isla Mujeres catamaran from Cancun) ($75 to $160, 6 to 8 hours) if you want sailing, a snorkel stop and an open bar more than time on the island: it gives you only an hour or two ashore.

## From Playa del Carmen or Tulum: the ruins and a cenote

:::schedule
7:00 am | Leave for Tulum
About 1 hour from Playa del Carmen. By rental car, bus or local minibus to the park entrance, then a shuttle or a ten-minute walk.

8:00 am | Tulum ruins at opening
Small, set on a cliff above the sea, about 90 minutes. By 10 am the site is full of tour groups and there is no shade.

10:00 am | A cenote
Several lie within twenty minutes of the ruins. The natural second half of the morning.

1:00 pm | Lunch, then the beach
:::

:::warn Tulum entry fees: bring up to 650 pesos
The ruins now lie inside Parque del Jaguar, and a visit can involve three separate charges: the archaeological site, the protected area and the park. The amounts were raised in January 2026 and reportedly cut again in July; websites still quote all the old figures. The ticket office at the park entrance is the only reliable source. The beach below the cliff is closed at times for nesting turtles, so don't count on swimming at the site.
:::

Prefer a guide? A [Tulum and cenote tour](book:Tulum ruins tour cenote) is worth it for the explanation: the site has few signs.

## With children: Xcaret instead

A large park built around natural inlets and caves, just south of Playa del Carmen. You float down underground rivers in a life vest, walk past jaguars, flamingos and sea turtles, and end with a two-hour evening show with hundreds of performers. The show is what people remember; leaving before it wastes much of the ticket.

| Ticket | What you get |
|---|---|
| Basic entry | The park, the rivers and the show. Food and snorkel gear extra |
| Plus | Adds a buffet meal, snorkel gear and a nicer changing area. Worth it for a full day |
| With transport | Adds a bus from your hotel; handy from Cancún, over an hour away |

Expect $100 to $160 per adult for [Xcaret tickets](book:Xcaret park tickets). Only biodegradable sunscreen is allowed in the water: bring your own, the park's price is high. Alternatives: Xel-Há, a natural lagoon with food and drinks included and no show, or cenotes on your own for a fraction of the price.

# Extra time | Beyond the resorts

A rental car for one or two days is the easiest way to reach these.

| Place | Why go | From |
|---|---|---|
| Cenotes near Puerto Aventuras | Clear water, few groups | Playa del Carmen, 20 minutes |
| Cobá | Jungle ruins around two lagoons; rent a bike at the entrance | Tulum, 45 minutes |
| Valladolid | Colonial town with the best regional food at local prices | On the way to Chichén Itzá |
| Ek Balam | A small Maya site with finely carved stucco | North of Valladolid |
| Puerto Morelos | Reef snorkeling from a fishing town | Between Cancún and Playa |
| Akumal | Sea turtles in a shallow bay | Between Playa and Tulum |
| Playa Delfines | Cancún's public beach | Cancún Hotel Zone |
| Punta Laguna | Spider and howler monkeys with a Maya community guide | Beyond Cobá; go early |
| Bacalar | A freshwater lagoon in seven shades of blue | Too far for a day; two nights at the end |

**One day by car:** Cobá in the morning, a cenote on the way back and Akumal late in the afternoon.

# Practical | Money, weather and staying well

## Money and tipping

- **Currency:** Pesos. Pay by card at hotels and larger restaurants and choose to be charged in pesos.
- **Tipping:** 10 to 15% in restaurants (check that service isn't already on the bill). At all-inclusives, a dollar or two (20 to 50 pesos) for bartenders, servers and housekeeping is appreciated. Tour guides and drivers: about 10% of the tour price.
- **Cash to carry on trips:** About 1,500 pesos per person covers entry fees, a cenote, lunch and tips.

## When to go

| Months | Weather | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| December to April | Dry, sunny, 80 to 85°F, cooler evenings | The best time and the highest prices; book months ahead for Christmas, Presidents' Day week and spring break |
| May to August | Hot and humid, around 90°F, afternoon showers | Good hotel prices; seaweed most likely |
| September to November | The wettest months | Heart of hurricane season and the cheapest; buy travel insurance that covers storms |

Hurricane season runs from June to November, with the highest risk in September and October.

## Staying well

- **Water:** Drink bottled or filtered water, including for brushing teeth outside resorts.
- **Sun:** It is stronger than at home. Wear a rash guard for snorkeling and you'll need far less sunscreen.
- **Sunscreen:** Only biodegradable, reef-safe sunscreen in cenotes, at Xcaret and on snorkel boats. Cenotes are part of an underground river system the whole peninsula drinks from.
- **Driving:** Stick to daylight, watch for speed bumps ("topes") in every village and keep small bills for tolls.
- **Power:** The same plugs and voltage as in the US.

- Passport valid for your whole stay
- Airport transfer booked; confirmation saved offline
- Chichén Itzá tour booked for day 3

- Biodegradable sunscreen, hat, rash guard, water shoes
