# Start here | The plan at a glance

New York is too big to "do", so this guide doesn't try. It gives each of four days one part of the city: Midtown, Lower Manhattan, Central Park with the museums, and Brooklyn with the High Line. You walk a lot, ride the subway between areas, and book only three things ahead: the Statue of Liberty ferry, one observation deck and a Broadway show. Much of the rest is free.

| Day | Area | The big booking | Walking |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Midtown: Grand Central, Fifth Avenue, Rockefeller Center, Times Square | Observation deck, Broadway | About 5 miles |
| 2 | Lower Manhattan: Statue of Liberty, Wall Street, 9/11 Memorial | Statue ferry, 9/11 Museum | About 5 miles |
| 3 | The Met, Central Park, Upper West Side | The Met (no slot needed) | About 6 miles |
| 4 | High Line, Chelsea, DUMBO, Brooklyn Bridge | Nothing | About 7 miles |

Expect 8 to 10 miles a day once you add wandering. Twenty street blocks make a mile.

:::booking Book in this order
1. **Three to four months ahead, if you want the crown:** the [Statue of Liberty](book:Statue of Liberty Ellis Island tickets) from Statue City Cruises, the only authorized seller. Crown access is tiny and goes first; pedestal access sells out weeks ahead; general admission (roughly $26 to $33) is easy until a few days before. Pick the first ferry of day 2.
2. **When your dates are fixed:** the one [Broadway show](book:Broadway show tickets New York) you must see, from its official seller. The biggest hits rarely discount. For anything else, wait and buy same-day (see day 1).
3. **A week ahead:** one [observation deck](book:New York observation deck tickets) for an hour before sunset on day 1. Sunset slots cost more and sell out first. Check the forecast the day before; most tickets can be moved for a fee.
4. **A week ahead:** the 9/11 Museum for the afternoon of day 2, roughly $33 to $36, and the Tenement Museum if you add it.
5. **Optional:** an [airport transfer](book:New York JFK airport transfer) for families and late arrivals.
:::

:::warn Days to plan around
- **Monday:** most Broadway theaters are dark. Don't put day 1 on a Monday if a show matters to you.
- **Wednesday and Saturday:** matinee days. An afternoon show frees your evening.
- **The Met:** closed on Wednesdays. Don't put day 3 on a Wednesday.
- **Monday evening:** the 9/11 Museum is free, with a limited number of tickets released online that morning.
:::

## Should you buy the CityPASS?

The New York CityPASS costs about $154 to $164 per adult and covers five attractions over nine days: the Empire State Building and the American Museum of Natural History, plus three you choose, such as the Statue ferry, the 9/11 Museum and Top of the Rock. Bought separately those five come to roughly $180 to $200.

- **Buy it** if you were going to visit at least four of the five anyway. In this plan: the Empire State Building (day 1), the Statue ferry and 9/11 Museum (day 2), and the Natural History Museum (day 3). Make the timed reservations through the pass the day you buy it.
- **Skip it** if you want Top of the Rock as your only deck, crown or pedestal access (not included), or a trip that is mostly neighborhoods, food and parks.
- **Passes sold by the day** only pay off at three or more paid sights a day, which leaves no time for the city itself.

## Where to stay

:::map Bases that make this plan easy
- **Midtown (roughly 34th to 59th Street):** Walk to day 1, a short subway ride to everything else, the most hotels. The practical choice for a first visit; avoid Times Square itself if you want to sleep.
- **Downtown (Greenwich Village, SoHo, Lower East Side):** Better food and nightlife, smaller hotels, higher prices.
- **Long Island City or Downtown Brooklyn:** One or two subway stops from Manhattan with noticeably lower rates and skyline views.
:::

Hotel prices peak in the first three weeks of December, during the UN General Assembly in late September and around the marathon on the first Sunday of November. Check for a nightly "destination" or "resort" fee before you book: many Manhattan hotels add one.

# Getting there | Three airports and your first evening

## Which airport?

| Airport | Best way to Manhattan | Time | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| JFK | Yellow taxi, flat fare | 45 to 75 minutes | $70 flat; $90 to $110 with tolls, surcharges and tip |
| JFK | AirTrain plus Long Island Rail Road | Around 40 minutes | About $14 to $16 |
| JFK | AirTrain plus subway | Around 1 hour | About $11.75 |
| LaGuardia | Free airport bus to the subway | About 45 minutes | $3 |
| LaGuardia | Taxi on the meter | 25 to 45 minutes | Roughly $40 to $60 |
| Newark | AirTrain plus train to Penn Station | About 30 minutes | Train fare |

- **From JFK:** With two or more people and luggage the taxi is worth it; alone, take the train. The flat fare covers anywhere in Manhattan below 96th Street. Join the official line outside the terminal and ignore anyone offering a ride inside. Ride apps are usually close to the taxi price and higher at busy times.
- **From LaGuardia:** There is no train. The free express bus runs from the terminals to the subway in Queens.
- **From Newark:** You are in New Jersey; taxis into Manhattan are the most expensive of the three airports. Take the train.
- **Prefer a driver waiting?** Book a [private transfer](book:New York JFK airport transfer).

:::tip Flying in from the West Coast
You gain a late night, not an early morning. Plan day 1 to start at 10 am and enjoy the fact that New York is still serving dinner at 10 pm.
:::

## Your first evening (free)

:::schedule
5:00 pm | Drop your bags, take the subway to 59th Street
Tap any contactless card or phone at the turnstile: $3 a ride. Use the same card every time; after a set number of paid rides in a week, the rest that week are free.

5:30 pm | Roosevelt Island Tramway
A red cable car from 59th Street and Second Avenue over the East River, with a view down the avenues, for a subway fare. Ride over, walk the waterfront for ten minutes, ride back at dusk.

7:30 pm | Dinner in your neighborhood
A slice of pizza folded in half counts.
:::

# Day 1 | Midtown

Today is the New York of the movies, all within a mile and a half: the great station, the library, Fifth Avenue, a view from above and Broadway after dark.

:::schedule
9:30 am | Grand Central Terminal
Free. Stand under the painted ceiling in the main concourse, then find the whispering gallery: the tiled arch outside the Oyster Bar on the lower level. Stand in opposite corners and whisper into the wall.

10:15 am | New York Public Library and Bryant Park
Five minutes west on 42nd Street. Walk past the stone lions and up to the Rose Main Reading Room. Bryant Park behind it has free chairs and good coffee kiosks.

11:15 am | Fifth Avenue north
Fifteen minutes to St. Patrick's Cathedral and Rockefeller Center across the street.

12:30 pm | Lunch
The food halls and delis in the side streets around Rockefeller Center are faster and cheaper than anything facing the plaza.

2:00 pm | Museum of Modern Art, or shopping
MoMA is on 53rd Street: Van Gogh's Starry Night, Monet's Water Lilies, Warhol. Two hours. Not a museum day? Fifth Avenue is right there.

4:00 pm | TKTS booth, if you don't have show tickets yet
Under the red steps in Times Square: same-day seats at up to 50% off. The line moves fast; choose from what is on the board.

One hour before sunset | Observation deck
Go up in daylight, watch the sun set and stay until the lights come on. See the comparison below.

7:00 pm | Times Square after dark
It only works at night, and ten minutes is enough.

8:00 pm | Broadway
Shows run 2 to 3 hours with an intermission. Eat before; the pre-theater menus on Ninth Avenue (Hell's Kitchen) are the best value near the theaters.
:::

## Which observation deck?

| Deck | From | Choose it for |
|---|---|---|
| Top of the Rock | About $45 | The best overall view: Central Park to the north, the Empire State Building in your photo. Open-air terraces |
| Empire State Building | $44 | The icon itself, an open-air 86th floor and a good museum. Quieter late at night |
| SUMMIT One Vanderbilt | Varies | Mirrored rooms; more experience than viewpoint. Great with teenagers. Wear pants: the floors reflect |
| Edge | About $36 to $45 | A glass floor over the street and the best sunset over the Hudson |
| One World Observatory | About $31 to $44 | The highest, indoors; harbor and bridges. Pairs with day 2 |

One deck is enough. First time in New York: Top of the Rock. If you bought a CityPASS, use the deck it includes and don't pay for a second. On a hazy or low-cloud day the view is poor at any height.

## Broadway for less

| Way to buy | Price | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Official seller, in advance | About $90 to $250, more for hits | The only reliable route for the most popular shows |
| TKTS booth | Up to 50% off | Same-day leftovers; long-running musicals appear most |
| Rush tickets | About $35 to $50 | Sold when the box office opens that day; usually two per person |
| Digital lottery | About $35 to $50 | Enter online the day before or that morning; have a plan B |

- **Buying at the theater's box office in person** avoids online fees, which can add $15 or more per ticket.
- **Where to sit:** The front of the mezzanine often beats the back of the orchestra for less money. "Partial view" is cheap for a reason; read what is blocked.
- **Never buy from someone on the street.**

# Day 2 | Lower Manhattan

Today starts on the water and ends where the city began. Go early: the ferry line grows through the morning.

:::schedule
8:00 am | The Battery
Take the subway to Bowling Green or South Ferry. Security is airport-style before boarding. Your tickets are on your phone or at the Statue City Cruises office in Castle Clinton, the round fort in the park.

8:30 am | First ferry to Liberty Island
Sit on the right side of the boat leaving Manhattan for the best view of the statue. Walk the grounds, visit the Statue of Liberty Museum and, if you booked it, go up the pedestal or climb the 162 narrow steps to the crown.

10:45 am | Ellis Island
Same ticket, next ferry stop. Twelve million immigrants were processed here; the museum deserves at least an hour. You're back in Manhattan around 1 pm.

1:30 pm | Lunch in the Financial District
Stone Street, a cobbled lane of old buildings with outdoor tables, is five minutes from the ferry.

2:30 pm | Wall Street on foot
The Stock Exchange, Federal Hall (where Washington was inaugurated) and Trinity Church, where Alexander Hamilton is buried, within three blocks.

3:15 pm | 9/11 Memorial
Free, no ticket. Two square pools fill the footprints of the Twin Towers, with the names of the nearly 3,000 people killed cut into the bronze edges. A white rose is placed on a name on that person's birthday.

3:45 pm | 9/11 Museum
Seven stories underground, at the foundations of the towers. Thorough and hard to walk through; allow two hours. Not suitable for young children.

6:00 pm | The Oculus
The white, winged transit hall beside the memorial. Free to walk through.

7:30 pm | Dinner in Chinatown, Little Italy or the West Village
:::

:::warn Avoid the street sellers
People in vests around Battery Park sell boat tickets that look official. Their boats circle the harbor but do not land on the island. Statue City Cruises is the only ferry that does.
:::

:::tip Only want the photo?
The Staten Island Ferry is free, runs around the clock and passes the statue. Ride it over and straight back: about an hour, and the skyline on the return is the best free view in the city.
:::

:::note At the memorial
This is a grave site for many families. Keep voices low at the pools, and do not sit on or lean over the names.
:::

# Day 3 | Central Park and the museums

A slower day: one great museum, a long walk through the park, and music at night.

:::schedule
10:00 am | The Metropolitan Museum of Art
It cannot be seen in a day, so pick two or three sections: the Egyptian Temple of Dendur, the European paintings, the American Wing or the rooftop (open in the warmer months). Three hours. Your ticket also covers The Cloisters on the same day.

1:00 pm | Lunch
Buy a sandwich on Madison Avenue and carry it into the park, or eat at the museum.

2:00 pm | Central Park, east to west
Enter at 79th Street. Walk south past the Conservatory Water (model sailboats) to Bethesda Terrace and its fountain, cross Bow Bridge, and loop through the Ramble to Strawberry Fields and the Imagine mosaic at 72nd Street. About 2 miles at park pace.

4:00 pm | American Museum of Natural History, or coffee
The museum is at 79th Street on the west side: dinosaurs and the blue whale. Tired of museums? The Upper West Side is the neighborhood of bagels and bookstores.

8:00 pm | Jazz
In Harlem or Greenwich Village. Small clubs charge a cover and a drink minimum; book the well-known ones ahead.
:::

:::map Getting your bearings
Avenues run north to south and streets run east to west. Fifth Avenue divides East from West. Central Park runs from 59th to 110th Street and is half a mile wide: crossing it takes 15 minutes, walking its length over an hour.
:::

# Day 4 | The High Line and Brooklyn

The last day is all outdoors: a park on an old rail line, a food market, and the walk back to Manhattan over the Brooklyn Bridge at sunset.

:::schedule
10:00 am | The High Line from Hudson Yards
Start at 34th Street and walk south, 1.5 miles above the streets of Chelsea. Free.

11:30 am | Little Island
A park on tulip-shaped columns over the Hudson, near the south end of the High Line. Free.

12:15 pm | Lunch at Chelsea Market
A food hall in an old cookie factory. Crowded at 1 pm; come a little early.

2:00 pm | Subway to Brooklyn
Take the A or C to High Street, or the F to York Street.

2:30 pm | DUMBO
Cobblestone streets under the Manhattan Bridge. The classic photo is on Washington Street, with the bridge framing the Empire State Building. Walk the waterfront park.

4:00 pm | Brooklyn Heights Promenade
Ten minutes on foot: a quiet walkway above the harbor facing Lower Manhattan.

One hour before sunset | Brooklyn Bridge, toward Manhattan
Walk it in this direction and the skyline is in front of you the whole way. Just over a mile; 30 to 40 minutes with photos. Stay in the pedestrian lane.

8:00 pm | Last dinner downtown
:::

## If it rains

Swap day 3's museums into the wet day. Move the observation deck (most tickets can be changed for a fee) and the Brooklyn Bridge walk to a clear one.

# Practical | Money, transport and weather

## What things really cost

- **Restaurant math:** Menu prices are before tax and tip. With sales tax of nearly 9% and a tip of 18 to 20%, the check lands almost 30% above the menu price.
- **Tipping:** 18 to 20% at sit-down restaurants, $1 to $2 per drink at a bar, 15 to 20% in taxis, a few dollars a day for hotel housekeeping. Counter service: your call.
- **Cheap and good:** Pizza by the slice, bagels, food carts, dumplings in Chinatown. A great lunch for under $10 still exists.

## Getting around

The subway runs all night and costs $3 a ride. Tap in; you don't tap out. Check whether your train is local or express, and whether the entrance says Uptown or Downtown before you go down the stairs. Walk whenever the trip is under 20 blocks. Yellow taxis are metered; ride apps surge in rain and at rush hour.

## Weather and when to go

| Season | Daytime temperature | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| April to June | 60 to 80°F | Blossom, outdoor tables; April can be cold and wet |
| July and August | Around 85°F, humid | Thunderstorms, free outdoor concerts, cheaper hotels |
| September to early November | 60 to 75°F | The best weather; fall color from mid-October |
| Mid-November to December | 40 to 50°F | Holiday season: magical, crowded, the most expensive |
| January to March | Around 39°F | Cold, quiet, the best hotel deals |

Thanksgiving week and New Year's Eve bring the biggest crowds. Times Square on December 31 means standing in a pen for hours without restrooms.

:::checklist Before you go
- Statue of Liberty ferry booked with Statue City Cruises
- One observation deck booked for sunset, day 1
- Broadway: tickets bought, or a plan for TKTS, rush or lottery
- 9/11 Museum timed ticket
- CityPASS reservations made, if you bought one
- A contactless card or phone wallet per person for the subway
- Comfortable shoes; layers in spring and fall
:::
