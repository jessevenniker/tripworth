# Start here | The plan at a glance

Barcelona is compact, sunny and run on its own clock: lunch at 2 pm, dinner after 8:30 pm, shops shut in the mid-afternoon. Fight that rhythm and you eat badly in empty restaurants; follow it and the city opens up. Three days is enough for the main sights if you book the Sagrada Família and Park Güell first and build around those two time slots. Day one is Gaudí, day two the old town and the sea, day three Montjuïc and a last look from above.

| Day | Area | The big booking | Walking |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Park Güell, Gràcia, Passeig de Gràcia, Sagrada Família | Park Güell, Sagrada Família | About 6 miles |
| 2 | Gothic Quarter, El Born, Barceloneta | Picasso Museum (optional) | About 6 miles |
| 3 | Montjuïc, one Gaudí house or Sant Pau, Bunkers del Carmel | One Gaudí house | About 5 miles |

Budget about €70 to €100 (roughly $80 to $115) per adult for tickets, depending on how many Gaudí interiors you add.

:::booking Book in this order
1. **Weeks ahead from spring to autumn:** the [Sagrada Família](book:Sagrada Familia tickets) for a late-afternoon slot on day 1, around 5 pm. €26 with the audioguide app, €30 with a guided tour, €36 with the towers.
2. **Right after, so the two slots fit together:** [Park Güell](book:Park Guell tickets) for the first slot on the same day, 9:30 am. €18. Leave at least three hours between the two.
3. **A week ahead:** one Gaudí house for day 3: [Casa Batlló or La Pedrera](book:Casa Batllo La Pedrera tickets). Casa Batlló's price climbs as the date gets closer.
4. **A few days ahead:** the Picasso Museum time slot for day 2, if you want it.
5. **Only with four days or more:** a [Montserrat day trip](book:Montserrat day trip from Barcelona).
6. **Optional:** an [airport transfer](book:Barcelona airport transfer) for families and late arrivals.
:::

:::warn Buy from the real sellers
Many look-alike websites sell Sagrada Família and Park Güell tickets at a markup. Buy on the official sites or from a seller you know. The Sagrada Família has no ticket office for same-day walk-ups in the busy months: no booking usually means no visit.
:::

## Where to stay

:::map Three bases that make this plan easy
- **Eixample:** The 19th-century grid, with wide sidewalks, Gaudí's houses and the best hotel choice. Quiet at night and well connected. The best base for a first visit.
- **Gothic Quarter and El Born:** Medieval lanes, tapas bars on your doorstep, some street noise until late. Choose El Born for the better restaurants.
- **Gràcia:** A former village of small squares. Local, lively, a metro ride from the old town.
:::

Your hotel will add a tourist tax at check-out. It doubled in April 2026 and now runs up to about €12 per person per night at five-star hotels, less at mid-range hotels and apartments. It is rarely in the price you see when you book.

# Getting there | Flights, the airport and your first evening

## Flying from the US

Barcelona-El Prat (BCN) has nonstop flights from several US cities, mostly in the summer season; year-round you can connect through Madrid or another European hub. From the East Coast the flight takes about 8 hours and lands in the morning, 6 hours ahead of New York. Long-haul flights generally arrive at Terminal 1.

## From the airport to the centre

| Option | Time | Price | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aerobús | About 35 minutes | €7.75 | Most visitors; runs every few minutes from both terminals |
| Taxi | 25 to 35 minutes | Roughly €35 to €40 | Three or more people; door to door |
| Train (R2 Nord) | 20 minutes to Sants | About €5 | Terminal 2 only; from Terminal 1 take the free shuttle first |
| Metro (L9 Sud) | About 50 minutes with a change | €5.90 | Hotels along its route |
| [Private transfer](book:Barcelona airport transfer) | 25 to 35 minutes | Fixed price | Families, late arrivals |

Official taxis are black and yellow and use the meter, with a minimum fare from the airport. The Aerobús stops at Plaça d'Espanya and Plaça de Catalunya.

:::tip Jet lag works in your favor here
You will be wide awake late at night, which is exactly when Barcelona eats. Nap for an hour after you land (set an alarm), then stay up for a proper 9 pm dinner.
:::

## Your first evening (no tickets needed)

:::schedule
5:00 pm | Passeig de Gràcia
Walk up the avenue. Casa Batlló and La Pedrera stand five minutes apart, and the façades are free. Casa Batlló shares its block with two showpiece houses by rival architects: a short history of Barcelona's Modernisme without a ticket.

6:30 pm | Vermouth hour
Locals drink vermouth with olives and chips before dinner. Any bar with barrels behind the counter will do.

8:30 pm | Tapas
Order two or three plates at a time and keep ordering. Bread rubbed with tomato (pa amb tomàquet) comes with everything.
:::

# Day 1 | Gaudí

Today pairs Gaudí's two great works at their best hours: the park at opening, before the heat, and the basilica in the late afternoon, when the low sun turns the nave red and orange.

:::schedule
9:00 am | Taxi to Park Güell
About ten euros from the centre and it drops you at the gate. By metro, it is line 3 to Lesseps or Vallcarca and a 15 to 20-minute walk, partly uphill.

9:30 am | Park Güell, first slot
You can enter up to 30 minutes after your slot starts. Gaudí's part is near the main entrance: the mosaic lizard on the staircase, the hall of columns and the terrace above it, edged by a long tiled bench with a view over the city to the sea. Allow 1.5 to 2 hours. There is no re-entry and little shade: bring water and a hat, and use the restrooms near the entrance first.

12:00 pm | Walk down through Gràcia
Twenty minutes downhill into a former village of small squares. Have a coffee on Plaça de la Virreina.

1:30 pm | Late lunch in Gràcia
Look for a weekday "menú del día": three courses with a drink for a fixed price, the best lunch deal in Spain.

3:30 pm | Passeig de Gràcia
If you skipped it last night, walk the avenue now. Otherwise go back to your hotel for a rest: the city does.

5:00 pm | Sagrada Família
Arrive 15 minutes early for security. Shoulders and thighs must be covered. Download the official app for the audioguide before you go. The stained glass on the west side is in warm reds and oranges, and in the two hours before closing the sun fills the nave with color. Allow 1.5 to 2 hours.

7:15 pm | Sant Pau from the outside
Walk ten minutes up the pedestrian Avinguda de Gaudí to the modernist hospital of Sant Pau, with the basilica behind you all the way. Terraces line the avenue.

9:00 pm | Dinner in Eixample
:::

## Which Sagrada Família ticket?

| Ticket | Price | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| Basilica with audioguide app | €26 | Most visitors |
| Basilica with guided tour | €30 | Worth the extra €4: the symbolism is dense and easy to miss |
| Basilica with towers | €36 | You want the view and don't mind a tight spiral stair |
| Guided tour and towers | €40 | Everything |

Under-30s pay €24 and under-11s are free. The best part of the Sagrada Família is the interior, and that is in every ticket. For the towers you go up by lift and come down a narrow spiral stair of several hundred steps, with no way to turn back; they close in rain and strong wind. The central tower was completed in 2026, making this the tallest church in the world. Work on the main façade continues.

:::note Official slots sold out?
[Guided tours](book:Sagrada Familia tickets) hold their own allocations and often still have places a few days out, at a higher price.
:::

# Day 2 | The old town and the sea

Today is on foot through 2,000 years: the Roman and medieval Gothic Quarter, the merchants' quarter of El Born, then the beach.

:::schedule
10:00 am | The Gothic Quarter
Start at the cathedral. Walk Carrer del Bisbe under its neo-Gothic bridge, find the Roman walls, and slip into Plaça de Sant Felip Neri: a small square with a fountain and a church wall pitted by a bomb in the Civil War.

12:00 pm | El Born
Cross Via Laietana. Santa Maria del Mar is a plain, soaring Gothic church built by the people of the port in 55 years. The Picasso Museum, in a row of medieval mansions, is five minutes away; book its time slot ahead.

2:00 pm | Lunch at the Santa Caterina market
A working food market under a rippling roof of colored tiles, calmer than the Boqueria on La Rambla.

3:30 pm | Palau de la Música Catalana (optional)
Five minutes away: a concert hall with a stained-glass ceiling like an upturned sun. Take the tour, or better, buy a ticket for a concert.

4:30 pm | Barceloneta
Walk 15 minutes to the beach and along the seafront. Swim from June to early October. Take only what you can keep an eye on.

6:30 pm | Rest

9:00 pm | Tapas in El Born
:::

:::warn Pickpockets
Barcelona's are skilled, and they work the metro, La Rambla and the beach. Carry your phone in a front pocket, keep bags zipped and in front of you, and never hang a bag on the back of a chair.
:::

:::map Today on the map
The cathedral to Santa Maria del Mar is under half a mile. The Picasso Museum, the Santa Caterina market and the Palau de la Música all sit within a 10-minute walk of each other. From El Born to the sand at Barceloneta is about three-quarters of a mile. Many shops and smaller sights close from about 2 pm to 5 pm, so plan the long lunch then.
:::

# Day 3 | Montjuïc and a last look

The hill above the port in the morning, a Gaudí interior in the afternoon, and the whole city at your feet at sunset.

:::schedule
10:00 am | Montjuïc by cable car or bus
Ride up to the castle for the view over the port, then walk down through the gardens.

12:30 pm | One museum on the hill
The Catalan art museum (MNAC), for Romanesque frescoes and the terrace view, or the Joan Miró Foundation. Pick one.

2:00 pm | Lunch in Poble-sec
At the foot of the hill, Carrer de Blai is lined with bars selling pintxos, small bites on bread, priced by the stick.

4:00 pm | The Gaudí house you booked
Or, for fewer crowds, the inside of Sant Pau: a hospital built as a garden city of tiled pavilions by Gaudí's contemporary Domènech i Montaner.

Sunset | Bunkers del Carmel
Old anti-aircraft batteries on a hilltop with the full panorama. Take a taxi up. Check the current opening hours first: evening access is restricted.

9:00 pm | Last dinner
:::

## Casa Batlló or La Pedrera?

| | Casa Batlló | La Pedrera (Casa Milà) |
|---|---|---|
| Price | From about €29, more close to the date | About €25 online, €29 at the door |
| Best for | The most striking interior; one photo-filled hour | The rooftop of warrior-like chimneys; how the building works |
| Feel | Tablet and light effects; busier | Calmer; includes a furnished period flat |
| Watch for | The basic ticket no longer includes the roof | Book online for the lower price |

If you have seen the Sagrada Família, one house is enough for most people. Both run evening visits with rooftop light shows at a higher price: atmospheric, but you see less of the architecture.

## A fourth day: Montserrat

A monastery set among saw-toothed rock, an hour away by train, with easy mountain walks. Worth it from four days in Barcelona; with three, stay in town.

- **On your own:** The R5 train from Plaça d'Espanya, then the rack railway or the cable car up. A combined ticket costs roughly €22 to €29. Allow 6 to 8 hours door to door.
- **[Half-day tour](book:Montserrat day trip from Barcelona):** Usually €50 to €80, with a coach and a guide, home by early afternoon.
- **Tour with winery:** Adds a cava or wine tasting; the most relaxed way to do it.
- **Skip it** on days of low cloud and on Sundays and holidays, when it is most crowded. It is several degrees cooler up there: bring a layer.

# Practical | Money, transport and local time

## Eating on local time

| Meal | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| Breakfast | Until 11 am | Coffee and a pastry or a small sandwich |
| Lunch | 1:30 to 3:30 pm | The main meal; look for the menú del día |
| Vermouth and snack | 6 to 8 pm | To bridge the gap |
| Dinner | From 8:30 pm | Kitchens are at their best after 9 |

A restaurant serving dinner at 6 pm is cooking for tourists.

## Money and tipping

- **Cards:** Tap-to-pay works nearly everywhere. Choose euros, not dollars, at the terminal.
- **Tipping:** Not expected. Round up or leave a euro or two; 5 to 10% for a very good dinner is generous.
- **Language:** Catalan and Spanish are both official. "Bon dia" (good day, Catalan) and "gracias" are both welcome.

## Getting around

The centre is compact and the metro is fast. A ten-trip card is enough for most three-day visits; each person needs their own. It is not valid on the metro to the airport. Taxis are metered and reasonable for hills like Park Güell and the Bunkers.

## Weather and when to go

| Season | Daytime temperature | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| March to May | 60 to 72°F | Mild and bright; showers in April |
| June to August | 80 to 86°F, humid | Beach weather; book weeks ahead; sightsee early and late |
| September and October | 72 to 80°F | September is the best month: warm sea, thinner crowds |
| November to February | Around 57°F | Sunny, quiet, cheapest; no swimming |

La Mercè, the city's main festival, fills the streets around 24 September. The mobile technology congress at the end of February or early March multiplies hotel prices for a few days. Many restaurants close for part of August.

## Plugs and phones

Spain uses 230 volts and round two-pin plugs (types C and F). Chargers handle the voltage; you need only an adapter.
