# Start here | The plan at a glance

London is the easiest big city in Europe for an American visitor and one of the most expensive. The good news: its best museums are free, and only two sights in this plan need a paid ticket booked ahead. Group the city by area and three days cover the essentials: Westminster and the royal parks on day one, the Tower and the South Bank on day two, and the British Museum plus one more of your choice on day three.

| Day | Area | The big booking | Walking |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Westminster, Buckingham Palace, Trafalgar Square, West End | Westminster Abbey | About 6 miles |
| 2 | Tower of London, Tower Bridge, South Bank, St Paul's | Tower of London, Sky Garden | About 6 miles |
| 3 | British Museum, then Greenwich, Harry Potter or Kensington | Depends on your option | 4 to 6 miles |

Expect 7 to 9 miles a day once you add wandering. Budget about £70 (roughly $90) per adult for the two paid sights; a West End show and day 3 extras come on top.

:::booking Book in this order
1. **Before anything else:** the UK ETA, the travel authorisation every US visitor now needs. £20 per person, children included, through the official UK ETA app or the UK government website only.
2. **If you want it, as early as possible:** the [Harry Potter Studio Tour](book:Harry Potter Warner Bros Studio Tour London tickets transfer) for day 3. There are no tickets at the door and popular dates sell out weeks or months ahead.
3. **Two to three weeks ahead:** the [Tower of London](book:Tower of London tickets Crown Jewels) for opening time on day 2, about £37.
4. **Same day:** [Westminster Abbey](book:Westminster Abbey tickets) for the first slot on day 1, £32. Not on a Sunday: the Abbey is open for services only.
5. **As soon as slots open:** the free Sky Garden for sunset on day 2. Slots are released a few weeks ahead on its own website and go fast.
6. **Optional:** a West End show, and a [day trip to Windsor, Stonehenge or Bath](book:Stonehenge Windsor Bath day trip from London) if you have a fourth day.
:::

:::warn Plan around these
- **Westminster Abbey:** no sightseeing on Sundays, and it closes to visitors for services and royal events. Check your date before you book flights around it.
- **Changing of the Guard:** does not take place every day, and on fewer days in winter. Check the schedule the week before.
- **Strikes:** a rail or Tube strike can disrupt a day. Look for announcements in the week before you travel.
:::

## Is the London Pass worth it?

For this plan, no. A one-day pass costs roughly £80 to £100 and you need about three major paid sights in a single day to break even. The Tower (about £37) plus the Abbey (£32) comes to £69, and the rest of this guide is free: the British Museum, the National Gallery, Tate Modern, the parks, the markets and the Sky Garden. Consider a multi-day pass only if you want to add several paid sights and Windsor Castle, and are happy to start at opening time every day.

## Where to stay

:::map Three bases that make this plan easy
- **Covent Garden and Soho:** Walk to the theatres, Trafalgar Square and the British Museum. The most fun and the most expensive.
- **South Bank and Southwark:** Across the river from the City, near Borough Market and Tate Modern, with good-value modern hotels.
- **Paddington and Bayswater:** Cheaper rooms, direct trains from Heathrow on the Elizabeth line, and Hyde Park at the end of the street.
:::

Anywhere within a five-minute walk of a Tube station in Zones 1 or 2 works. London hotel rooms are small; check the size if it matters.

# Getting there | The ETA, Heathrow and your first evening

## The ETA

- **Who needs it:** Every visitor who does not need a visa, including US citizens. Each traveler needs one, children included.
- **How to get it:** Only through the official UK ETA app or the UK government website. It costs £20, is linked to your passport and is valid for two years.
- **When:** Most decisions come within minutes, but apply at least three days ahead.
- **Watch out:** Look-alike websites charge several times the official fee for the same application.

## Flying from the US

Most US flights land at Heathrow. From the East Coast the flight takes about 7 hours, and London is 5 hours ahead of New York. Overnight flights land in the morning; your room probably won't be ready, so ask the hotel to hold your bags.

## From Heathrow to central London

| Option | Time | Price | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Elizabeth line | About 35 minutes | £15.50 | Most hotels; room for luggage |
| Piccadilly line (Tube) | 50 to 60 minutes | £5.90 | Lowest budget, light bags |
| Heathrow Express | 15 minutes to Paddington | About £25 | Hotels near Paddington |
| Black cab | 45 to 90 minutes | About £60 to £100 | Late arrivals |
| [Pre-booked car](book:London Heathrow airport transfer) | 45 to 90 minutes | Usually less than a cab | Families with luggage |

You do not need a ticket or an Oyster card. Tap the same contactless card or phone on the yellow reader when you enter and when you leave, and the system charges the right fare, with a daily maximum. Each person needs their own card or device.

:::tip Beat the jet lag
Book nothing with a time slot for arrival day. Walk in daylight, eat an early pub dinner and be in bed by 9:30 pm local time.
:::

## Your first evening (no tickets needed)

:::schedule
3:00 pm | Drop your bags and walk to the river
Head for Westminster Bridge for your first look at Big Ben and Parliament.

4:00 pm | The South Bank walk
Follow the river east past the London Eye toward Waterloo Bridge: street performers, book stalls under the bridge and the skyline across the water. About a mile, all flat.

6:00 pm | Pub dinner
Order at the bar, give your table number and pay when you order. Nobody will come to take your order at the table in a traditional pub.
:::

# Day 1 | Westminster and the royal parks

Today is royal and political London, all within a mile and a half: the Abbey, Parliament, the parks, the Palace, then art and theatre.

:::schedule
9:15 am | Westminster Tube station
Step out and Big Ben is directly above you.

9:30 am | Westminster Abbey, first slot
Every coronation since 1066 has taken place here. The multimedia guide is included. The route passes the Coronation Chair, the tombs of Elizabeth I and Mary, Queen of Scots, and Poets' Corner; the cloisters and the Chapter House are included too. Allow an hour and a half.

11:00 am | St James's Park to Buckingham Palace
A 15-minute walk along the lake. On the days it takes place, the Changing of the Guard starts around 11 am, and the railings are packed long before. Watching the guards march up The Mall is easier than fighting for a spot at the gates.

1:00 pm | Lunch near Trafalgar Square
Walk down The Mall and through Admiralty Arch. The crypt café under St Martin-in-the-Fields is a reliable, reasonably priced lunch.

2:15 pm | National Gallery
Free. Van Gogh's Sunflowers, Turner, Monet and Leonardo in one building. Pick one wing and give it 90 minutes.

4:30 pm | Covent Garden
Ten minutes on foot. Street performers on the piazza, and Neal's Yard, a hidden courtyard of brightly painted buildings, five minutes north.

5:30 pm | Early dinner
Most West End restaurants offer a pre-theatre set menu before 6:30 pm.

7:30 pm | A West End show
Same-day tickets are sold at a discount at the booth in Leicester Square; for a show you have your heart set on, book ahead.
:::

## Seeing the Abbey for free

Attending a service costs nothing. Evensong, sung by the Abbey choir on most weekday afternoons, lets you sit in the quire for 45 minutes. You cannot walk around or take photos, but you hear the building used as it was meant to be.

:::tip Abbey first, not last
Opening hours are shorter than at most sights, and last entry is mid-afternoon. That is why it opens day 1.
:::

:::map Add-ons within five minutes
- **Churchill War Rooms:** The underground wartime headquarters, with its own ticket. Swap it for the National Gallery if history beats art for you.
- **Westminster Bridge:** The best photo of Big Ben and Parliament, especially early.
- **Buckingham Palace State Rooms:** Open to visitors only in summer, from July to September.
:::

# Day 2 | The Tower and the South Bank

Today follows the river from the medieval fortress to the modern skyline. Everything after the Tower is free.

:::schedule
8:45 am | Tower Hill Tube station
Be at the gate before opening. A weekday is calmer than a weekend.

9:00 am | Tower of London: Crown Jewels first
Walk straight to the Crown Jewels: by late morning that line is the longest in the fortress. Then join the next Yeoman Warder tour from the entrance (an hour with one of the Beefeaters who live here; included, funny and the best introduction to the place), and finish with the White Tower and a walk along the walls. Allow three hours.

12:15 pm | Tower Bridge
Walk across at street level for free. The glass walkway up top needs a separate ticket.

12:45 pm | Riverside walk to Borough Market
Twenty minutes west along the south bank, past HMS Belfast and The Shard. Eat your way around the stalls for lunch. The market is quietest early in the week, and closed on Mondays.

2:30 pm | Tate Modern
Free. Modern art in a former power station; take the lift to the viewing level for the view of St Paul's.

4:00 pm | Millennium Bridge to St Paul's Cathedral
Walk across the footbridge straight to the dome. Entering the cathedral is a paid ticket; attending evensong is free.

5:30 pm | Leadenhall Market and St Dunstan in the East
Fifteen minutes east: a covered Victorian market with a painted iron roof, then a garden growing inside the ruins of a bombed church. Both are free.

Sunset | Sky Garden
A free viewing garden at the top of a City skyscraper, two minutes from St Dunstan. The view rivals The Shard; you only need the booking. Bring ID and allow time for security.
:::

:::note No Sky Garden slot?
Walk-up entry is sometimes possible on weekday mornings, and the bars and restaurants at the top take separate reservations. The paid alternative is the view from [The Shard](book:The Shard London tickets).

# Day 3 | The British Museum and one more

Start with the world's history under one roof, then choose the afternoon that fits you.

:::schedule
10:00 am | The British Museum
Free; booking a free timed ticket online gets you in faster. Go first to the Rosetta Stone (room 4) and the Parthenon sculptures (room 18) before the rooms fill, then the Egyptian mummies upstairs. Two hours is plenty.

12:15 pm | Sir John Soane's Museum (optional)
A 10-minute walk. The house of an architect who filled every wall with paintings, casts and an Egyptian sarcophagus. Free, small, often with a short line; check the opening days.

1:00 pm | Lunch, then your option
:::

## Option A: Greenwich by riverboat

Take the river bus from Westminster or Embankment pier (tap your contactless card; about 40 minutes downstream, passing under Tower Bridge). In Greenwich, climb the hill to the Royal Observatory to stand on the prime meridian and look back at the skyline, then walk down past the Cutty Sark. Return by boat or the DLR train.

## Option B: Harry Potter Studio Tour

This is not a theme park: there are no rides. It is the studio where the films were made, with the original sets, costumes and props: the Great Hall, Diagon Alley, the Hogwarts Express and the huge model of the castle. It takes 3.5 to 4 hours plus travel, so swap the British Museum to another morning or keep it to an hour.

| How to go | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ticket only | £58.50 adult, £47 ages 5 to 15 | Train from Euston to Watford Junction (about 20 minutes), then the shuttle bus |
| [Ticket with coach from London](book:Harry Potter Warner Bros Studio Tour London tickets transfer) | From about £94 | Often available when the official site is sold out |

Sold out? Check the official site for returns a few days ahead, and try late slots on school days. Butterbeer is sold halfway through; share one first, it is very sweet.

## Option C: South Kensington and Hyde Park

The Victoria and Albert Museum, the Natural History Museum and the Science Museum stand side by side and are all free. Pick one, then walk north into Hyde Park and along the Serpentine. The best option with children, and in rain.

| Trip | Cost | Verdict |

| Windsor Castle by train | About £31 to £36, 30 to 50 minutes each way | The best for most; half a day, great with children. Check opening days |
| Bath by train | About 1 hour 20 minutes from Paddington | The most relaxed: a Georgian city, the Roman Baths and a proper lunch |

# Practical | Money, transport and etiquette

## Money and tipping

- **Cards:** London is nearly cashless. Tap-to-pay works everywhere, including buses and market stalls; many places no longer take cash. Choose to pay in pounds, not dollars, when a terminal asks.
- **Restaurants:** A service charge of about 12.5% is usually already on the bill. If it is, you don't need to add more. If not, 10 to 12% is normal.
- **Pubs:** No tipping at the bar.
- **Taxis:** Round up to the nearest pound.

## Getting around

Tap in and out with the same card on the Tube; on buses tap once when boarding. Fares are capped per day, so you never pay more than a day pass would cost. Stand on the right on escalators and let people pass on the left: it is the one rule Londoners enforce. Look right first when crossing the street.

## Weather and when to go

| Season | Daytime temperature | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| March to May | 50 to 63°F | Parks in bloom; sun and showers in one day |
| June to August | 68 to 75°F | Daylight after 9 pm; hot Tube; peak prices |
| September to November | 55 to 66°F | September is as good as summer with fewer visitors |
| December to February | 43 to 48°F | Christmas lights in December; dark by 4:30 pm; cheapest in January |

It can rain in any month, usually briefly. Pack a light waterproof jacket instead of an umbrella.

## Plugs and phones

- **Plugs:** The UK uses 230 volts and a three-pin plug (type G) that is different from the rest of Europe. Chargers handle the voltage; you need a UK adapter.
- **Phone:** Check your carrier's day pass, or buy an eSIM before you fly.
