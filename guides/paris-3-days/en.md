# Start here | The plan at a glance

Paris is bigger than Rome and flatter than it looks on a map, and its famous sights sit in clusters along the river. Three days is enough for the main ones if you do two things: plan around the closing days, and book three time slots before you fly. This guide spends day one on the Eiffel Tower and the river, day two on the Louvre and the islands, and gives you a choice for day three: Versailles, or the Musée d'Orsay and Montmartre.

| Day | Area | The big booking | Walking |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Trocadéro, Eiffel Tower, Champs-Élysées, the Seine | Eiffel Tower, cruise | About 6 miles |
| 2 | Louvre, Palais Royal, the islands, Left Bank | Louvre | About 6 miles |
| 3A | Versailles | Palace of Versailles | About 7 miles |
| 3B | Musée d'Orsay, Montmartre | None required | About 5 miles |

Expect 7 to 9 miles a day once you add wandering. Budget €130 to €150 (roughly $150 to $175) per adult for tickets: since 2026 the Louvre and Versailles charge visitors from outside Europe more than European residents.

:::booking Book in this order
1. **90 days ahead, when lift tickets go on sale:** the [Eiffel Tower](book:Eiffel Tower tickets summit) for a morning slot on day 1. Buy on the official site first; summit slots sell out before anything else. €36.70 by lift to the summit, €23.50 to the second floor.
2. **Next:** the [Louvre](book:Louvre Museum tickets) for 9 am on day 2, €32 for visitors from outside Europe. A slot is not strictly required, but without one you can wait an hour or more.
3. **If you choose Versailles:** the [Passport ticket with the 9 am Palace slot](book:Versailles Palace tickets from Paris) for day 3. €35 from April to October, €25 in winter.
4. **A few days ahead:** a one-hour [Seine cruise](book:Seine river cruise Paris) for sunset on day 1 or your arrival evening. Pick a ticket you can move if it rains.
5. **Free but worth reserving:** Notre-Dame (a free reservation shortens the wait) and Sainte-Chapelle's time slot.
6. **Optional:** a [private transfer from CDG](book:Paris CDG airport transfer) for families and late arrivals.
:::

:::warn Plan around the closing days
- **Louvre:** closed on Tuesdays. Open late, until 9 pm, on Wednesdays and Fridays.
- **Versailles and the Musée d'Orsay:** closed on Mondays.
- **1 May:** most museums close. **14 July:** the national holiday, busy everywhere.
- **First Sunday of the month:** some museums are free, which means lines.
:::

## Should you buy the Paris Museum Pass?

For American visitors the pass now pays off sooner than it used to, because single tickets cost you more while the pass costs everyone the same. The two-day pass is about €85. The Louvre (€32) and Versailles (€35) alone come to €67; add the Musée d'Orsay and Sainte-Chapelle and you are past €85.

- **Buy it** if you do the Louvre, Versailles and at least two more included sights on two consecutive days. In this plan that means days 2 and 3A.
- **Skip it** if you choose option 3B, or prefer to wander. Under-18s get into most of these museums free anyway, so buy it for adults only.
- **It does not include** the Eiffel Tower or cruises, and you still have to reserve the (free) time slots at the Louvre, Versailles and Sainte-Chapelle.

## Where to stay

:::map Three bases that make this plan easy
- **The Marais (3rd and 4th arrondissements):** Old streets, falafel and bakeries, bars at night, and a walk to Notre-Dame and the Louvre.
- **Saint-Germain (6th):** Classic Left Bank cafés and bookshops, a short walk to the Musée d'Orsay and the river. Quieter and pricier.
- **Near the Louvre and Palais Royal (1st and 2nd):** The most central, with metro lines in every direction and the covered passages on your doorstep.
:::

Hotel rooms in Paris are small by American standards. A room of 180 square feet for two is normal; check the size before you book if it matters to you.

# Getting there | Flights, the airport and your first evening

## Flying from the US

Most US flights land at Charles de Gaulle (CDG), northeast of the city. From the East Coast the flight takes about 7 to 8 hours, and Paris is 6 hours ahead of New York. Overnight flights arrive in the morning, often before your room is ready: ask the hotel to hold your bags.

## From CDG to the city

| Option | Time | Price | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| RER B train | 35 to 45 minutes | €14 | Solo travelers and couples with light bags |
| Official taxi, Right Bank | 45 to 70 minutes | €56 flat, per car | Two or more with suitcases |
| Official taxi, Left Bank | 45 to 70 minutes | €65 flat, per car | Hotels south of the Seine |
| [Private transfer](book:Paris CDG airport transfer) | 45 to 70 minutes | From about €70 | Families, child seats, late arrivals |

- **The flat taxi fare is fixed by law** and includes luggage. The Seine decides whether your hotel counts as Right Bank or Left Bank. A taxi booked in advance adds a €7 reservation charge.
- **Take taxis only from the official rank** outside the terminal. Ignore drivers who approach you inside.
- **The airport train needs its own airport ticket,** loaded on a Navigo Easy card or in your phone wallet. An ordinary metro ticket is not valid, and inspectors fine on the spot.
- **The RoissyBus to Opéra no longer runs:** it was discontinued on 1 March 2026. Older guidebooks still list it.
- **Landing at Orly instead?** It is closer. Metro line 14 runs to the centre on the same airport ticket, and taxis have their own lower flat fares.

:::tip Beat the jet lag
Don't book the Louvre for the day you land. Stay outside in daylight, keep moving and take the sunset cruise below: you sit down, the city glides past, and you see how it all fits together.
:::

## Your first evening (one easy booking)

:::schedule
4:00 pm | Check in, then a walk through your neighborhood
Buy a baguette sandwich or a pastry from the nearest bakery. Say *bonjour* before anything else.

6:00 pm | Square du Vert-Galant
Walk to the Pont Neuf and take the steps down to the tip of the Île de la Cité, at water level. This is where Parisians sit with a bottle at sunset.

Sunset | One-hour Seine cruise from Pont Neuf
About €15 to €20. Sit on the open deck: under a glass roof, photos pick up reflections. The boats pass the Louvre, the Musée d'Orsay, the Eiffel Tower and Notre-Dame. Book a [sightseeing cruise](book:Seine river cruise Paris) you can reschedule.

8:30 pm | Dinner, early by Paris standards
Restaurants fill from 8 pm. A set menu ("formule") is usually the best value.
:::

# Day 1 | The tower and the river

Today runs from the Trocadéro across the river to the tower, then east along the Seine and up the Champs-Élysées. About 6 miles on foot, or less with one metro ride.

:::schedule
8:45 am | Trocadéro terrace
Take the metro to Trocadéro and walk out onto the terrace: this is the classic photo of the tower, and in the morning the sun is behind you. Then walk down through the gardens and across the bridge.

9:30 am | Eiffel Tower
Arrive 20 minutes before your slot. Everyone passes the same security check, so no ticket skips that line. With a summit ticket you change lifts on the second floor; the wait for the second lift can add 30 to 45 minutes. Allow 2 to 3 hours in all.

12:30 pm | Lunch on Rue Cler
A market street ten minutes' walk from the tower, with cheese shops, bakeries and café terraces. In good weather, buy a picnic and eat it on the Champ de Mars.

2:30 pm | The river to Pont Alexandre III
Walk the quay east past Les Invalides to the most ornate bridge in Paris. The Petit Palais, just across it, is a free fine-arts museum with a garden café few visitors find.

4:00 pm | Champs-Élysées and the Arc de Triomphe
Walk up the avenue, or take metro line 1 two stops if your feet protest. Reach the arch through the underpass (never cross the traffic circle) and climb it for the view down twelve avenues. It is a paid ticket, included in the Museum Pass.

Sunset | Seine cruise, if you didn't take it yesterday
Boats near the Eiffel Tower suit today's route. The tower sparkles for five minutes on the hour after dark.

8:30 pm | Dinner in your own neighborhood
:::

## Which Eiffel Tower ticket?

| Ticket | Price | Who it's for |
|---|---|---|
| Lift to the summit | €36.70 | Your one visit, the full experience |
| Stairs to 2nd floor, lift to summit | €28 | Lift tickets gone; 674 steps, easier than they sound |
| Lift to the second floor | €23.50 | The best view for the money |
| Stairs to the second floor | €14.80 | Cheapest, often the shortest line |

Go to the top for the experience of having been there, not for a better photo. From the summit, 906 feet up, the city flattens into a map; from the second floor you look across at Sacré-Cœur, the Arc de Triomphe and the Seine. The summit closes in strong wind.

:::note Official tickets sold out?
Try the stairs ticket first: it often has places when the lift doesn't. [Guided tours and resellers](book:Eiffel Tower tickets summit) hold their own tickets and cost more.
:::

# Day 2 | The Louvre and the islands

Today is the historic heart: the Louvre in the morning, then the two islands in the Seine and dinner on the Left Bank. Don't schedule this day on a Tuesday.

:::schedule
8:40 am | Carrousel du Louvre entrance
Skip the line at the Pyramid. The underground entrance through the shopping arcade at 99 rue de Rivoli is usually faster and under cover.

9:00 am | The Louvre: three hours with a plan
The museum holds about 35,000 works; don't try to see them all. Go straight to the Mona Lisa in the Denon wing while the room is calmest, then the Winged Victory on the staircase and the Venus de Milo below. Spend what is left on one section you care about, such as the Egyptian rooms or the French crown jewels. A [guided tour](book:Louvre Museum tickets) uses the group entrance and does the navigating for you.

12:30 pm | Lunch near the Palais Royal
Walk through the arcades and the garden of the Palais Royal, then five minutes north to Galerie Vivienne: a shopping arcade from the 1820s with mosaic floors and a glass roof.

3:00 pm | Sainte-Chapelle
On the Île de la Cité. Fifteen stained-glass windows, each nearly 50 feet tall, best on a sunny afternoon. Reserve a time slot; security is airport-style because it sits inside the courts complex.

4:15 pm | Notre-Dame
Five minutes away. The cathedral is free; a free online reservation shortens the wait. Walk around the back for the view of the flying buttresses from the river.

5:30 pm | Latin Quarter
Cross to the Left Bank. Ten minutes' walk brings you to the Arènes de Lutèce, a Roman amphitheater hidden behind apartment buildings where locals play boules.

8:00 pm | Dinner in Saint-Germain or the Latin Quarter
Avoid the restaurants with photo menus on Rue de la Huchette. Two streets further, the same money buys a far better meal.
:::

:::tip The quiet Louvre
Wednesday and Friday evenings, when the museum stays open until 9 pm, are the calmest hours of the week. If your dates allow, move the Louvre to an evening and do the islands in the morning. Last entry is one hour before closing. Children under 18 are free, but book them a free time slot too.
:::

:::map Today on the map
The Louvre to the Palais Royal is 3 minutes on foot; on to Galerie Vivienne 5 more. From there to Sainte-Chapelle is about a mile, across the Pont Neuf. Sainte-Chapelle, Notre-Dame and the Latin Quarter all lie within half a mile of each other.
:::

# Day 3 | Versailles, or Orsay and Montmartre

Choose by your interests and by the calendar. Versailles is closed on Monday; so is the Musée d'Orsay, so neither option works on a Monday. If day 3 falls on one, swap it with day 1.

## Option A: Versailles

:::schedule
7:45 am | RER C from central Paris
Roughly 40 minutes to Versailles Château Rive Gauche, then a 10-minute walk. Buy a return ticket to avoid the evening line at the machines.

9:00 am | The Palace, first slot
Be at the gate early. By 11 am the Hall of Mirrors is shoulder to shoulder. The free audio guide is in the official app; download it on hotel Wi-Fi.

11:00 am | The gardens
Walk down the central axis toward the Grand Canal. On fountain-show days in high season the gardens need a ticket, which the Passport covers.

12:00 pm | Lunch by the Grand Canal
Then rent a bike or a golf cart if walking the whole estate is too much: it is far larger than it looks on the map.

1:30 pm | The Trianon estate
Two smaller palaces and Marie-Antoinette's hamlet, a 25-minute walk from the Palace. It opens at noon.

4:30 pm | Train back
You are in Paris by early evening.
:::

- **On your own:** Cheapest. Train plus Passport.
- **[Guided tour with transport](book:Versailles Palace tickets from Paris):** Two to three times the price, with the coach from Paris and a guide through the Palace.
- **Bike tour:** A full day with a market picnic by the canal, the most relaxed way to see the grounds.

## Option B: Orsay and Montmartre

:::schedule
9:30 am | Musée d'Orsay
The Impressionists, in a former train station. Start on the top floor with Monet, Renoir and Van Gogh and work down. Two hours.

12:30 pm | Lunch, then the metro to Montmartre
Get off at Abbesses or Anvers.

2:00 pm | Sacré-Cœur and the back streets
The basilica is free and the view from its steps covers the whole city. Then leave the crowds on Place du Tertre behind: walk down Rue Lepic and around the vineyard on Rue des Saules.

5:00 pm | Canal Saint-Martin
Iron footbridges, locks and tree-lined quays, 15 minutes away by metro. On Sundays the roads along it close to cars.
:::

## Your last evening

Walk through the Marais after dark, when the mansions are lit and the squares are quiet. Place des Vosges is the oldest planned square in Paris and one of the most beautiful.

Many small museums close on Monday or Tuesday. Check the day before you cross the city for one.

## If it rains

- **The covered passages** near the Palais Royal were built for exactly this weather.
- **Move the Louvre or the Musée d'Orsay** to the wet day and the tower to the dry one. The summit is no fun in cloud.
- **Reschedule the cruise.** Rain changes the experience completely.

# Practical | Money, transport and etiquette

## Money and tipping

- **Cards:** Tap-to-pay works almost everywhere, including bakeries. If a terminal offers to charge you in dollars, choose euros.
- **Tipping:** Service is included in every restaurant bill by law ("service compris"). Leave a euro or two in coins for good service, or round up; 5 to 10% is generous. Nobody expects 20%.
- **Water:** Ask for "une carafe d'eau" and you get free tap water. Bottled water is charged.

## Getting around

The metro is fast and covers everything in this guide. Load tickets on a Navigo Easy card or in your phone wallet. Keep your ticket until you exit: inspectors fine on the spot. Paris is also one of the great walking cities; the river is your compass.

## Manners that change your trip

Greet with *bonjour* (or *bonsoir* in the evening) before anything else, in every shop, café and bakery. It changes the service you get more than any tip. Speak quietly in restaurants and on the metro, and ask for the check when you want it: bringing it unasked is considered rude.

## Weather and when to go

| Season | Daytime temperature | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| April to early June | 55 to 72°F | Gardens in bloom; pack a layer and an umbrella |
| Mid-June to August | Around 77°F | Daylight until 10 pm; crowded; some restaurants close in August |
| September and October | 60 to 72°F | Parisians are back, the light is at its best |
| November to March | 40 to 50°F | Cheapest and quietest; Versailles costs €10 less |

## Plugs, phones and safety

- **Plugs:** France uses 230 volts and round two-pin plugs (type E). Phone and laptop chargers handle the voltage; you need only an adapter.
- **Pickpockets:** Work the metro, the Trocadéro, the tower and Sacré-Cœur. Ignore petitions, friendship bracelets and "found" gold rings.
- **Phone:** Check your carrier's day pass, or buy an eSIM before you fly.

:::checklist Before you fly
- Passport valid for at least three months after your return date
- Check the EU entry rules for US citizens on travel-europe.europa.eu
- Eiffel Tower, Louvre and (option A) Versailles slots booked
- Free reservations for Notre-Dame and Sainte-Chapelle
- Card without foreign transaction fees
- Plug adapter, umbrella, comfortable shoes
- Official Versailles app downloaded
:::
