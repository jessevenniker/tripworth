# Start here | The plan at a glance

Aruba is small, safe, dry and almost always sunny: 20 miles end to end, outside the hurricane belt, with 80 to 90°F every month of the year. That makes it an easy island to do badly, by never leaving the hotel strip. A week works best as four beach days and three days out: a snorkeling trip, a day in Arikok National Park, and a day driving the south of the island. Book the snorkeling catamaran and the 4x4 tour before you fly, and rent a car for two days rather than the whole week.

| Day | Plan | The big booking | Car needed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arrive, beach, sunset | Nothing; taxi from the airport | No |
| 2 | Eagle Beach and Palm Beach | None | No |
| 3 | Snorkeling catamaran to the Antilla wreck | Catamaran | No |
| 4 | Arikok National Park and the Natural Pool | 4x4 tour | No |
| 5 | The north: lighthouse, chapel, Boca Catalina | Rental car, day 1 | Yes |
| 6 | The south: Mangel Halto, Savaneta, San Nicolas, Baby Beach | Rental car, day 2 | Yes |
| 7 | Last swim, Oranjestad, fly home | Be at the airport 3 hours early | No |

:::booking Book in this order
1. **Months ahead in high season (Christmas to Easter):** your hotel. Rates peak then and the best rooms on Eagle Beach go first.
2. **Up to seven days before you fly:** the online ED card, Aruba's entry form, on the official Aruba site only. The card is free; most visitors arriving by air pay a $20 sustainability fee with it. Every traveler needs one, children included (under-8s are exempt from the fee).
3. **Before you fly:** a morning [snorkeling catamaran to the Antilla wreck](book:Aruba snorkeling catamaran Antilla) for day 3. About $60 to $100 for three hours with gear and drinks. Check that "Antilla" is in the description.
4. **Before you fly:** a [4x4 tour to the Natural Pool in Arikok](book:Aruba Arikok National Park Natural Pool tour) for the morning of day 4. Ask whether the $22 park fee is included; often it is not.
5. **A few days ahead:** a rental car for days 5 and 6, picked up near your hotel.
6. **Only if the photo matters:** a Flamingo Beach day pass (see *Extra time*). Passes are few and often sell out.
:::

:::tip No car at all?
Days 5 and 6 work without one. Replace day 5 with a half-day [island tour](book:Aruba island tour) of the north coast and the lighthouse, and day 6 with a taxi or bus to Baby Beach and San Nicolas. You lose Mangel Halto's flexibility and gain two drinks at lunch.
:::

## Where to stay

:::map Two beaches, two moods
- **Eagle Beach:** The wide, quiet one, with low-rise hotels across a small road, the two famous divi-divi trees and room to spread out. Best for couples and anyone who came for the beach itself.
- **Palm Beach:** The lively one, a mile and a half north, lined with high-rise resorts, piers, water sports, restaurants and bars. Best for families who want everything on the doorstep and for nightlife. Most catamarans leave from its piers.
- **Oranjestad:** The capital, with cheaper rooms and the Renaissance resort (and its private island), but no great beach on the doorstep.
:::

The two beaches are a 30-minute walk or a short bus or taxi ride apart, so you can sleep on one and eat on the other.

# Getting there | Flights, the entry card and the airport

## Flying from the US

Queen Beatrix International Airport (AUA) has nonstop flights from many US cities: about 3 hours from Miami, 4.5 from New York, Newark and Boston, 4 from Atlanta and Charlotte. Aruba is on Atlantic Standard Time all year: the same as New York in summer, one hour ahead in winter.

- **Passport:** You need a valid US passport.
- **Money:** US dollars are accepted everywhere, at a fixed rate, and prices are often listed in dollars. You never need to change money.
- **Power:** The same plugs and voltage as at home.
- **Water:** Aruba's tap water is distilled seawater and excellent. Bring a refillable bottle.

## From the airport to your hotel

Taxis have no meters. The government sets a fixed fare per destination, per car, for up to four passengers with one suitcase and one carry-on each.

| To | Fare per car |
|---|---|
| Oranjestad | $26 |
| Eagle Beach hotels | $37 |
| Palm Beach hotels | $41 |
| Extra bags | $3 each |
| Sundays, holidays and 11 pm to 7 am | $5 surcharge |

Pay in US dollars, in cash unless the driver confirms cards first. Uber and Lyft do not operate in Aruba. A [pre-booked transfer](book:Aruba airport transfer) makes sense for groups of five or more.

## Flying home: three hours early

Aruba has US preclearance: you go through US immigration and customs before boarding and land in the US as a domestic passenger. It saves time on arrival but adds a second set of lines in Aruba. Be at the airport three hours before departure. The busiest period is Friday to Sunday from late morning to mid-afternoon; if you can choose, fly home midweek.

# Days 1 and 2 | Settle in

## Day 1: arrive

:::schedule
Afternoon | Check in and walk straight to the beach
Sunscreen first. Aruba is only twelve degrees from the equator, and the breeze hides how strong the sun is: you burn in twenty minutes without noticing.

Sunset | On the sand
Aruba's hotel beaches face west, so every evening ends over the water. Sunset is between about 6:30 and 7:15 pm all year.

Evening | Dinner near your hotel
Reserve for the popular beachfront tables; walk-ins work nearly everywhere else.
:::

## Day 2: Eagle Beach and Palm Beach

:::schedule
8:00 am | Eagle Beach
The sand is cool and nearly empty. Walk to the two divi-divi trees, bent by the trade wind, for the photo before the crowd.

10:00 am | Settle under a palapa
The thatched shade huts on the public beach are free but taken early. Many hotels reserve theirs for guests.

1:00 pm | Lunch out of the sun

4:30 pm | Walk north to Palm Beach
About 30 minutes along the shore and the path. Watch the water sports, pick a pier bar for sunset.

7:30 pm | Dinner on Palm Beach
The strip behind the high-rises has the island's widest choice of restaurants. Take a taxi back.
:::

## The beaches, ranked for a week's stay

| Beach | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Eagle Beach | Space, soft sand, sunsets | Free, wide, the island's best all-rounder |
| Baby Beach | Children and floating | A shallow lagoon at the southern tip; day 6 |
| Arashi | Snorkeling and locals | The last beach before the lighthouse; lovely at sunset |
| Boca Catalina | Snorkeling from shore | Small, calm, plenty of fish; go early |
| Mangel Halto | Snorkeling among mangroves | Wooden decks into a lagoon; stay inside the reef |

| Surfside | A quick dip in town | 15 minutes' walk from the cruise port |

# Days 3 and 4 | Water and wilderness

## Day 3: snorkeling catamaran

:::schedule
8:30 am | Check in at the pier on Palm Beach
Put on reef-safe sunscreen before you board. Morning trips have calmer water and better visibility.

9:00 am | Three hours on the water
Two or three stops, usually the Antilla wreck, Boca Catalina and Arashi. Gear and drinks are included.

12:30 pm | Lunch on Palm Beach

Afternoon | Pool and shade
You will be more tired from the sun than you expect. Plan nothing.
:::

**The Antilla** was a German cargo ship. Her crew sank her on purpose on May 10, 1940, the day Germany invaded the Netherlands, to keep her out of Dutch hands. She lies in Malmok Bay, 398 feet long and up to 60 feet deep, one of the largest wrecks in the Caribbean. Parts of the hull come close to the surface, so snorkelers see her from above without diving.

| Option | Price | Choose it if |
|---|---|---|
| [Catamaran with open bar](book:Aruba snorkeling catamaran Antilla) | About $60 to $100 | You want the classic: music, snacks, a rope swing on some boats. Boats carry 50 or more |
| Small boat, up to about 12 people | More | You want more time in the water and a guide who points things out |
| From shore, on your own | Gear rental only | You're a confident swimmer. Boca Catalina, Arashi and Mangel Halto are free. You will not reach the Antilla from the beach |

Aruba is windy; trips can be rerouted on rough days.

## Day 4: Arikok National Park

Almost a fifth of the island is national park: cactus, divi-divi trees, wild goats and donkeys, and a north coast of pounding surf. Nothing like the hotel strip.

:::schedule
8:00 am | Pickup by 4x4
Start early, before the heat. Wear a swimsuit and water shoes; bring water and a hat.

9:30 am | The Natural Pool (Conchi)
A pool behind a wall of volcanic rock, filled by waves breaking over the edge. The rocks are sharp and slippery: water shoes make the difference between enjoying it and limping back.

11:00 am | Caves and coves
Most tours add the Fontein or Quadirikiri caves, with rock drawings and shafts of daylight through the roof, and a look at Boca Prins or Dos Playa: dramatic coves with white dunes, too rough to swim.

1:00 pm | Back at the hotel
Lunch, then the beach.
:::

| How to visit | Verdict |
|---|---|
| [4x4 jeep tour](book:Aruba Arikok National Park Natural Pool tour) | The easy way to the Natural Pool; a driver handles the rough track |
| Rental 4x4 | Only if you're used to rocky tracks, and only if your rental contract allows it; many do not |
| Normal rental car | Fine for the paved road to the visitor center, the caves and Boca Prins. Not the Natural Pool |
| On foot | A hot, exposed hike. Go with a guide and start early |

:::warn Before you go
Entry is $22 per adult, free under 17, and ticket sales stop mid-afternoon. The pool closes to swimmers when the sea is rough, and nobody can promise it will be open on your day. The ride is bumpy: not for bad backs or pregnancy.
:::

# Days 5 and 6 | The rest of the island by car

Pick up the car near your hotel on the morning of day 5 and return it on the evening of day 6. Driving is on the right, roads are good and the island is hard to get lost on: the sea is always close.

## Day 5: the north

:::schedule
8:30 am | Alto Vista Chapel
A small yellow chapel alone on a hill on the north coast. Quiet early in the morning.

9:30 am | Bushiribana ruins
The stone shell of a 19th-century gold smelter, facing the surf. Ten minutes along the coast road.

10:30 am | California Lighthouse
The northern tip, with a view over the dunes and the whole hotel coast.

11:15 am | Tres Trapi and Boca Catalina
Three steps cut into the rock lead into clear water where sea turtles often feed. Boca Catalina, next door, is the easiest shore snorkeling on the island.

1:00 pm | Lunch on the way back

3:00 pm | Fisherman's Huts
Watch the kitesurfers and windsurfers at the north end of Palm Beach.

Sunset | Arashi Beach
The last beach before the lighthouse. Locals come here when the hotel beaches are full.
:::

## Day 6: the south

:::schedule
9:00 am | Mangel Halto
Thirty minutes from the hotels. A calm lagoon between mangroves, with wooden decks to enter the water and some of the best shore snorkeling on the island. Go in the morning while the water is calm.

12:00 pm | Fish shack in Savaneta
The catch of the day, fried and served on a pier, paid by weight.

1:30 pm | San Nicolas
The island's second town, with dozens of large murals. Walk the main street and the blocks around it.

3:00 pm | Baby Beach
A wide, shallow lagoon at the southern tip. You can walk far out with the water at your waist.

5:30 pm | Drive back
About 45 minutes to Palm Beach. Return the car.
:::

:::warn At Mangel Halto
Stay inside the reef unless you are a strong swimmer. The current outside it is stronger than it looks.
:::

On either day, the Donkey Sanctuary is a short detour: volunteers care for the island's donkeys, visiting is free, and children can feed them.

# Day 7 | Last day

:::schedule
8:00 am | One more swim
Rinse and pack your swimsuit last.

10:30 am | Oranjestad
Pastel Dutch-style buildings along the waterfront and the main street, and the last shopping. An hour or two is enough.

Three hours before departure | At the airport
You clear US customs and immigration in Aruba. Eat before or after the lines, not between them.
:::

# Extra time | Flamingo Beach, a cruise day and more

## Is Flamingo Beach worth it?

Only if the flamingo photo matters to you. It sits on Renaissance Island, a private island owned by the Renaissance resort in Oranjestad. Hotel guests go for free; everyone else needs a day pass of roughly $125 per adult, sold in small numbers (reportedly released weekly on Saturday morning) and often sold out.

- **For two or more:** compare one night at the hotel with the price of your passes. It can cost little more.
- **Good to know:** there are only a handful of flamingos, on one of the island's two beaches, which has its own rules about children. Families should check with the hotel first. Take the first boat: by late morning there is a line for the photo.
- **Skip it** if beach days are limited. Eagle Beach is free, wider and more beautiful.

## In port for one day?

The ship docks in downtown Oranjestad, a 5 to 10-minute walk from the main street.

- **Cheapest good day:** a taxi to Eagle Beach (about 10 minutes), rent chairs, and head back two hours before all-aboard.
- **On the water:** a snorkeling catamaran with port pickup.
- **See the island:** a half-day [4x4 or island tour](book:Aruba shore excursion) of the north coast.
- **Cruise passengers** pay no ED card fee. Check ship time against island time when you step off.

## More for a free half day

- **Kitesurfing or windsurfing lesson** at Fisherman's Huts; the wind is strongest from May to October.
- **Sunset sail** from Palm Beach.
- **Hooiberg:** climb the 500-odd steps of the cone-shaped hill in the middle of the island early in the morning.

# Practical | Money, weather and the sun

## Money and tipping

- **Currency:** The Aruban florin, but US dollars are accepted everywhere and ATMs dispense both. Cards work at hotels, restaurants and shops; carry cash for taxis, fish shacks and beach chairs.
- **Tipping:** Many restaurants add a service charge of 10 to 15% to the bill. Check before you tip: it does not always go straight to your server, so a few dollars extra for good service is kind. Without a service charge, 15 to 20% as at home.
- **Costs:** Nearly everything is imported. Expect restaurant and grocery prices at or above big-city US levels.

## Weather and when to go

| Months | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Mid-December to mid-April | High season. Hotels fill and rates peak; book months ahead |
| Mid-April to August | The same sun at lower prices. The trade winds are strongest, which keeps the heat comfortable |
| September to mid-December | The quietest period. The wind can drop, which makes it feel hotter; short showers from October |

Temperatures stay between 80 and 90°F all year, and only about 18 inches of rain fall annually. Aruba sits far south, close to Venezuela, below the usual path of Atlantic hurricanes: one of the safest Caribbean choices from June to November.

## Getting around without a car

- **Bus:** Public buses run between Oranjestad, Eagle Beach and Palm Beach all day.
- **Taxi:** Fixed fares per car. Agree on the fare before you get in.
- **Rental car:** Two days is enough. Rent near your hotel rather than at the airport for the whole week: two airport taxi rides cost about $80, less than five idle rental days.

## Language

Dutch and Papiamento are the official languages; nearly everyone also speaks English and Spanish. "Bon dia" (good morning), "danki" (thank you) and "dushi" (sweet, lovely) will earn you a smile.

- Valid passport
- ED card completed on the official Aruba site, sustainability fee paid
- Snorkeling catamaran booked for day 3
- Arikok 4x4 tour booked for day 4
- Rental car reserved for days 5 and 6
- Reef-safe sunscreen, hat, rash guard, water shoes
- Refillable water bottle
- Cash in small US bills for taxis and beach chairs
