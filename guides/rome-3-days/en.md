# Start here | The plan at a glance

Rome rewards a plan more than almost any city in Europe. The two big sights, the Colosseum and the Vatican Museums, run on timed tickets that sell out, and the city is laid out so that a little geography saves you miles of walking. This guide groups each day by area: ancient Rome on day one, the Vatican and the historic centre on day two, fountains, the Borghese Gallery and Trastevere on day three.

| Day | Area | The big booking | Walking |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Colosseum, Forum, Monti, Capitoline | Colosseum, first slot | About 6 miles |
| 2 | St. Peter's, Vatican, Navona, Pantheon | Vatican Museums | About 7 miles |
| 3 | Trevi, Borghese, Aventine, Trastevere | Borghese Gallery | About 8 miles |

Expect 8 to 10 miles a day on foot once you add wandering, and budget roughly €70 (about $80) per person for the timed tickets in this plan.

:::booking Book in this order
1. **As soon as your dates are fixed:** [Vatican Museums timed ticket](book:Vatican Museums Sistine Chapel tickets) for the morning of day 2, about €25 online. A guided or early-entry tour is the alternative if you want the Sistine Chapel without the crush.
2. **Same day:** [Borghese Gallery](book:Borghese Gallery tickets) for 9 am on day 3. Entry is by two-hour slot only, and weekends go first.
3. **Exactly 30 days before day 1:** the [Colosseum](book:Colosseum Rome tickets) for the first slot of the morning. Official tickets are released about 30 days ahead and summer slots go within hours. Want the arena floor or the underground? Book the [Full Experience or an underground tour](book:Colosseum underground tour) instead.
4. **Optional:** a [private airport transfer](book:Rome Fiumicino airport transfer) if you land with children or late at night, and a [catacombs and Appian Way tour](book:Rome catacombs Appian Way tour) if you add a fourth morning.
:::

:::warn Plan around three closures
- **Vatican Museums:** closed on Sundays, except the last Sunday of the month, which is free and packed. Don't put day 2 on a Sunday.
- **Borghese Gallery:** closed on Mondays. Don't put day 3 on a Monday.
- **First Sunday of the month:** state museums, including the Colosseum, are free. That sounds good and means the worst crowds of the month. Avoid it for day 1.
:::

## Which order for the days?

The order in this guide works for most weeks, but swap days freely to fit the closures above. The best sequence for a Thursday arrival, for example, is Friday Colosseum, Saturday Vatican, Sunday Borghese (open), with your arrival afternoon spent on the easy evening walk in the *Getting there* chapter.

## Where to stay

:::map Three bases that make this plan easy
- **Monti:** Behind the Forum, with small streets, wine bars and a short walk to the Colosseum and to Termini station for the airport train. The best base for this plan.
- **Centro Storico (around the Pantheon and Navona):** Everything on foot, beautiful at night, and the priciest rooms per square foot.
- **Trastevere:** Across the river, lively, full of restaurants and loud after dark. The FL1 airport train stops at Trastevere station.
:::

Staying near Termini saves money and is handy for the airport train, but the streets right around the station are the least pleasant in the centre. If you stay there, pick the Monti side.

# Getting there | Flights, the airport and your first evening

## Flying from the US

Rome's main airport, Fiumicino (FCO), has nonstop flights from several US hubs, including New York, Newark, Boston, Atlanta, Chicago and Miami. Flights from the East Coast take about 8 to 9 hours and arrive in the morning, Rome time, which is 6 hours ahead of New York.

:::tip Beat the jet lag
Don't schedule a timed ticket on the day you land. Drop your bags, take a shower and get outside into daylight. Walk the easy evening loop below, eat early and go to bed at a normal local hour. Day 1 then starts fresh.
:::

## From Fiumicino to the centre

| Option | Time | Price | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Leonardo Express train | 32 minutes to Termini | About €14 | Staying near Termini or Monti |
| FL1 regional train | About 45 minutes | About €8 | Staying in Trastevere |
| Official white taxi | 45 to 60 minutes | €55 fixed, per car | Two or more people with luggage |
| Airport bus | 55 minutes or more | About €7 | Lowest budget |
| [Private transfer](book:Rome Fiumicino airport transfer) | 45 to 60 minutes | From about €60 | Families, late arrivals |

- **The taxi fare is fixed:** €55 per car to addresses inside the old city walls, for taxis licensed by the city of Rome. Taxis licensed in the town of Fiumicino can charge more, so confirm the fare before you get in.
- **Ignore anyone who offers a taxi inside the terminal.** Use the official rank outside.
- **Paper train tickets must be stamped** in the green machines on the platform before boarding, or you risk a fine.

## Your first evening (no tickets needed)

:::schedule
4:00 pm | Check in, shower, go outside
Daylight is the best cure for jet lag. Grab an espresso standing at a bar counter: it costs about half what you pay at a table.

5:30 pm | Pantheon
About €7, paid at the door on most weekdays; open until 7 pm with last entry at 6:30 pm. Thirty minutes inside is enough. Look up at the oculus, the open hole in a dome that is nearly 2,000 years old.

6:30 pm | Piazza Navona
Five minutes on foot. Three fountains, street painters and the best people-watching in Rome. Don't eat on the square itself.

7:30 pm | Early dinner, early night
Eat a few streets away from any big sight. Romans dine at 8:30 pm or later, so at 7:30 pm you'll get a table without booking.
:::

# Day 1 | Ancient Rome

Today is a single area: the Colosseum, the Roman Forum and the Palatine Hill share one ticket, and the old neighbourhood of Monti is just behind them. You'll walk about 6 miles.

:::schedule
8:00 am | Breakfast near the Colosseum, then the security line
Be at the entrance 20 to 30 minutes before your slot. Bring the photo ID on which the ticket was booked: the names are checked.

8:30 am | Colosseum, first slot
The first slot beats the heat and the groups. A standard visit takes about an hour. With a [Full Experience ticket or underground tour](book:Colosseum underground tour) you also stand on the arena floor or walk the tunnels where animals and fighters waited, about 30 to 45 minutes more.

10:00 am | Roman Forum and Palatine Hill
Same ticket. Enter on the Colosseum side, walk up the Palatine first for the view down into the Circus Maximus, then descend into the Forum and exit near the Capitoline. Allow two to three hours. There is almost no shade, so carry water.

1:00 pm | Lunch in Monti
The streets around Piazza della Madonna dei Monti are full of small trattorias and bakeries. Order a classic Roman pasta (carbonara, amatriciana or cacio e pepe) and sit down for an hour: you've earned it.

2:30 pm | Basilica of San Clemente (optional)
Five minutes from the Colosseum. A 12th-century church built on a 4th-century church, built on a Roman house and temple, and you walk down through all three. A few euros for the lower levels. Many small churches close from about noon to 3 pm, so check the hours on the door.

3:30 pm | Capitoline Hill
Climb Michelangelo's ramp to the square. Walk behind the town hall for the free terrace view over the whole Forum. The Capitoline Museums are here too, if you still have the energy for the bronze she-wolf and Marcus Aurelius.

6:00 pm | Rest
Go back to your room for an hour. Day 1 is the longest in the plan.

8:30 pm | Dinner, then the Colosseum by night
Eat in Monti, then walk back past the Colosseum when it is lit. It is the best free view of the trip.
:::

## Tickets for today

| Ticket | Price | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Standard (24 hours) | About €18 | Colosseum plus Forum and Palatine |
| Full Experience Arena | About €24, valid 2 days | Adds the arena floor and extra Forum sites |
| Full Experience Underground and Arena | About €6 more on a ticket | Adds the tunnels below the arena |
| Small-group tour | Usually €50 to €70 | 3 hours with a guide, Forum included |
| Underground tour | Usually €90 to €130 | The tunnels with a guide |

:::tip Is a guide worth it here?
For a first visit, yes. The Colosseum explains itself; the Forum is a field of ruins that means little without someone telling you what stood where. Choose a group of 15 or fewer with headsets, check that the Forum is included, and pick free cancellation up to 24 hours ahead. Meeting points are outside the site, so allow 15 minutes to find yours.
:::

:::note Sold out on the official site?
That doesn't mean sold out everywhere. Tour companies hold their own allocations, so a [guided tour or reseller ticket](book:Colosseum guided tour Roman Forum) is often still available at a higher price. If the underground is gone, the arena floor is the best second choice: you look straight down into the corridors from the edge of the platform.
:::

# Day 2 | The Vatican and the historic centre

Today crosses the river. The Vatican Museums and St. Peter's Basilica have entrances 15 minutes apart on foot, and the walk back over the Tiber leads straight into the most beautiful part of the old city.

:::schedule
7:45 am | St. Peter's Square
The basilica is free and has its own security line, which builds fast. Be there early and you're inside in minutes. Shoulders and knees must be covered: no shorts or sleeveless tops, for men or women.

8:00 am | St. Peter's Basilica
Michelangelo's Pietà is just inside on the right. Climbing the dome costs extra and is paid on the spot; the last part is a narrow, sloping staircase, so skip it if you dislike tight spaces.

10:00 am | Vatican Museums and Sistine Chapel
Walk around the walls to the museum entrance (about 15 minutes). Your timed ticket includes the Sistine Chapel at the end of a one-way route; allow three hours. Download an audio guide before you go, or take a [guided tour](book:Vatican Museums Sistine Chapel tickets) that picks the rooms that matter out of miles of galleries.

1:30 pm | Late lunch in Prati
The streets east of the museums, away from the tourist menus by the walls, are where office workers eat. Look for a place with a short handwritten menu.

3:30 pm | Castel Sant'Angelo and the bridge of angels
Walk 15 minutes to the round fortress and cross the Ponte Sant'Angelo. The castle is a paid museum with a fine rooftop terrace if you want one more ticket today.

4:30 pm | Piazza Navona and the Pantheon
If you skipped them on arrival day, this is the time. From the bridge it's a 10-minute walk to Navona and 5 more to the Pantheon.

6:00 pm | Aperitivo
Order a spritz around the Pantheon or Campo de' Fiori. Many bars put out small snacks with an early-evening drink.

8:30 pm | Dinner near Campo de' Fiori
The market square is touristy by day and lively at night. The side streets towards the river are where the better tables are.
:::

## The Vatican, step by step

- **Timed ticket:** About €25 online (€20 plus booking fee). Includes the museums and the Sistine Chapel.
- **St. Peter's Basilica:** Free, separate entrance. Never pay anyone for "skip-the-line basilica tickets" outside.
- **Best entry time:** The first slot fills with tour groups. If you can't get a morning slot, after 2 pm is usually calmer: morning groups have left and the museums stay open into the evening. In that case, swap the order and do the basilica afterwards.
- **Avoid:** Mondays (other Rome museums are closed, so everyone comes here), the free last Sunday of the month, and Wednesday mornings, when the papal audience fills the square.
- **In the Sistine Chapel:** No photos and no talking. Guards enforce both, so put your phone away and look up.

:::map The walk from the Vatican to the Pantheon
St. Peter's to Castel Sant'Angelo is about half a mile down Via della Conciliazione. Cross the bridge, keep straight for Via dei Coronari (antique shops), and you reach Piazza Navona in 10 minutes. The Pantheon is 5 minutes east, Campo de' Fiori 5 minutes south. Total: under 2 miles, mostly car-free.
:::

# Day 3 | Fountains, art and Trastevere

Today starts early at the Trevi Fountain, spends the late morning with Bernini in the Borghese Gallery and ends across the river in Trastevere. It's the longest walk of the trip, about 8 miles, so wear your best shoes.

:::schedule
7:30 am | Trevi Fountain
At this hour it is nearly empty and you can sit on the edge. By 10 am the crowd is ten deep. Toss your coin with your right hand over your left shoulder, as tradition says.

8:00 am | Spanish Steps
Ten minutes on foot. Sitting on the steps is not allowed and the fines are real, so look and keep walking. Have breakfast in one of the side streets.

9:00 am | Borghese Gallery
Your two-hour slot is strict: arrive 30 minutes early to collect tickets and check bags (large bags must be checked). Bernini's sculptures on the ground floor are the reason you came; the paintings upstairs include several Caravaggios.

11:00 am | Villa Borghese gardens and the Pincio terrace
Walk through the park to the Pincio for the view over Piazza del Popolo and the domes. Rent a bike or a pedal cart if your legs are tired.

12:30 pm | Lunch around Piazza del Popolo
Then take a taxi or the bus south; the Aventine is 3 miles away.

3:00 pm | Aventine Hill
The Orange Garden terrace gives a wide view over the river and the domes. Next door, look through the keyhole of the Knights of Malta gate: St. Peter's dome appears, framed by a garden hedge. There may be a short line; it moves fast.

4:00 pm | Testaccio (optional)
Walk down the hill to the Testaccio market (where Romans buy lunch) and the Protestant Cemetery, where Keats and Shelley are buried beside an ancient pyramid. Check the market hours: stalls close in the afternoon.

6:30 pm | Trastevere
Cross the river and wander. Santa Maria in Trastevere has gold mosaics that glow in the evening.

8:30 pm | Last dinner
Go deep into the lanes, away from the main square. The further you are from the fountain in Piazza di Santa Maria, the better the value.
:::

:::tip Free water all day
Rome's public fountains, the little iron "nasoni", run with cold drinking water. Carry a bottle and refill it for free. Put a finger under the spout and the water shoots up from a hole on top.
:::

# Extra time | Hidden gems and a fourth day

Have a free afternoon, or a fourth day? These are the places most visitors walk past. All are free or cost a few euros, none needs a reservation, and each is within half an hour of the Colosseum.

| Place | Why go | Fits with |
|---|---|---|
| Aventine keyhole | St. Peter's dome framed by a hedge | Day 3 afternoon |
| Orange Garden | Wide view, best at sunset | Day 3 afternoon |
| San Clemente | Three layers of history underground | Day 1, after the Colosseum |
| Appian Way | The original Roman road, catacombs | Its own half day |
| Centrale Montemartini | Marble statues among old turbines | Testaccio |
| Quartiere Coppedè | Fairy-tale Art Nouveau streets | A taxi ride north |
| Testaccio market | A Roman stew sandwich for lunch | Day 3 |
| Santo Stefano Rotondo | A round 5th-century church | Celio, near day 1 |
| Protestant Cemetery | Keats, Shelley and a pyramid | Day 3 |

## A fourth morning on the Appian Way

The original Roman road is still paved with basalt and lined with tombs. It is closed to cars on Sundays, the best day to rent a bike and ride it. Visit one of the catacombs on the way: entry is by guided visit only. A [catacombs and Appian Way tour](book:Rome catacombs Appian Way tour) handles transport, which helps, because the road starts beyond the old city walls.

## If it rains

- **Swap the order:** Do the Vatican Museums or the Borghese on the rainy day and the Forum on a dry one.
- **The Pantheon in the rain:** The oculus is open to the sky, so it rains inside and the floor drains it away. Worth seeing once.
- **San Clemente and the Capitoline Museums:** Both are indoors and rarely crowded.

# Practical | Money, transport and etiquette

## Money and tipping

- **Cards:** Contactless cards and phones work almost everywhere. Carry €20 to €40 in cash for small bars, markets and church donation boxes. Choose to pay in euros when a terminal offers to convert to dollars: the conversion rate is worse.
- **Tipping:** Not expected the way it is at home. Many bills include a cover charge (coperto) per person. Round up or leave €1 to €2 per person for good service; 10% is generous.
- **Coffee:** Standing at the counter is cheaper than sitting at a table, sometimes by half.

## Getting around

Central Rome is small. From the Colosseum to the Pantheon is a 25-minute walk, and most visitors use transit less than they expect. Tap a contactless card at the metro gates. A 72-hour transport ticket costs €22.

:::note Is the Roma Pass worth it?
Usually not. The 72-hour pass costs €62.90 and covers two sights plus transport, but not the Vatican, and you still have to reserve a Colosseum slot. The Colosseum (about €18) plus the Borghese (about €18) plus a 72-hour transport ticket (€22) comes to €58 bought separately. Buy the pass only if you stay outside the centre and ride transit four or more times a day.
:::

## Weather and when to go

| Season | Daytime temperature | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| April to early June | 65 to 80°F | The best balance; Easter week is busy |
| Mid-June to August | 85 to 95°F and up | Sightsee before 11 am and after 5 pm |
| September and October | 70 to 80°F | Warm, thinner crowds from late September |
| November to March | 50 to 60°F | Cheapest and quietest; November is the rainiest |

In August many family-run restaurants close for their own holidays.

## Plugs, phones and safety

- **Plugs:** Italy uses 230 volts and round two- or three-pin plugs (types C, F and L). Phone and laptop chargers handle the voltage; you only need an adapter. Hair straighteners from the US often don't.
- **Phone:** Check your carrier's international day pass, or buy an eSIM before you fly.
- **Pickpockets:** Work crowded buses, the metro and the busiest squares. Keep your phone in a front pocket and your bag zipped and in front of you.
- **Dress code:** Shoulders and knees covered for St. Peter's, the Vatican Museums, the Pantheon and every church. A light scarf solves it.
